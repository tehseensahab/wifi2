The TP-Link Deco X50 is a dual-band Wi-Fi 6 mesh kit. It is a reasonable buy for a home on an internet plan of up to 1 Gbps that has no need for the 6 GHz band, and a poor fit for a multi-gig plan. We don't quote a sale price, because we could not confirm one on an official page on November 14, 2026.

*Correction, November 14, 2026: an earlier version of this page reported a $149 price for Prime members and said we had checked the price history. We could not verify either claim, so both have been removed. This is a research-based page built from TP-Link's US product page. We have not tested the Deco X50.*

## What is the Deco X50?

| Specification | What TP-Link's US page lists |
|---|---|
| Wi-Fi | Wi-Fi 6, dual-band, AX3000 class |
| 5 GHz | 2402 Mbps (802.11ax, 160 MHz channels) |
| 2.4 GHz | 574 Mbps (802.11ax) |
| Ethernet | 3 gigabit ports per unit, WAN/LAN auto-sensing |
| Coverage (3-pack) | Up to 6,500 sq ft |
| Security | WPA3-Personal supported |
| HomeShield | Free Basic plan; fees apply for the Pro plan |
| Backhaul | Optional Ethernet backhaul between units |
| Hardware versions | V1 and V2 have two internal antennas; V3 has three |

The speed figures are maximum link rates for each band, not the internet speed you will see. Coverage is TP-Link's own figure, and real homes with walls and floors get less.

## Who is the Deco X50 a good fit for?

- **Plans up to 1 Gbps.** Every port is gigabit, so the kit can't pass more than 1 Gbps from your modem. On a faster plan, look at a system with 2.5G ports; see [do you need a multi-gig router]({{< relref "/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router" >}}).
- **Homes where you can wire the units.** TP-Link lists optional Ethernet backhaul. A dual-band system shares its radios between your devices and the link between units, so a cable helps most here. Our [tri-band vs. dual-band mesh guide]({{< relref "/guides/tri-band-vs-dual-band-mesh" >}}) explains why.
- **Buyers who don't need 6 GHz.** The X50 has no 6 GHz radio. If your phones and laptops are Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7, read [Wi-Fi 6 vs. Wi-Fi 6E]({{< relref "/guides/wifi-6-vs-wifi-6e" >}}) before settling on a Wi-Fi 6 kit.

## How do I tell whether a Deco X50 price is a real deal?

1. **Check the pack size.** The X50 is sold as a 1-pack, 2-pack and 3-pack, and listings often show the smallest pack's price first.
2. **Check the hardware version.** TP-Link lists V1 to V3 with different antenna counts. Ask the seller if the listing doesn't say.
3. **Compare the price per unit** against a current Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 kit, not against a crossed-out "was" price.
4. **Count the subscription.** HomeShield's Pro plan costs extra. Decide whether you want it before comparing against systems that bundle security.
5. **Know the regulatory picture.** Our [TP-Link state lawsuits explainer]({{< relref "/news/tp-link-state-lawsuits-what-owners-should-know" >}}) and [FCC conditional approval tracker]({{< relref "/news/fcc-router-conditional-approvals-status" >}}) cover what owners and buyers should know.

## Bottom line

Buy a Deco X50 on sale if your plan is 1 Gbps or slower, you can live without 6 GHz, and the per-unit price beats newer kits by a wide margin.
