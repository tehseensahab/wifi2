**To use your own modem with Xfinity Voice, you need a voice modem, which Xfinity calls an eMTA, with phone ports, and Xfinity's My Device Info tool has to show that it works with your service.** A data-only cable modem has no phone ports, so it can't carry the line. Retail voice modems such as NETGEAR's CM2050V and ARRIS's SURFboard T25 are built for Xfinity Voice, but Xfinity doesn't guarantee customer-owned eMTAs, and your phone stops working in a power outage unless you add a battery backup.

This guide is research-based, drawn from Xfinity's support pages and NETGEAR and ARRIS product pages checked on November 21, 2026. We haven't tested these modems. We also don't list prices: the retailer listings we found today were out of date or no longer sold the item new. For the wider question of whether owning a modem saves money at all, see [should I buy my own modem?](/guides/should-i-buy-my-own-modem/).

## What does Xfinity Voice need from my modem?

Xfinity's requirement is short: "To use Xfinity Voice, you'll need a compatible embedded Multimedia Terminal Adapter (eMTA)." An eMTA is a cable modem with telephone ports built in. You can rent one from Xfinity, or use your own if it's compatible. If you rent a gateway instead, Xfinity says gateways need a voice port, and "The XB9 Xfinity Gateway does not support Xfinity Voice services."

Three conditions come with owning one:

- **Xfinity doesn't guarantee it.** "We do not guarantee the compatibility of Xfinity Voice with any other service, system or equipment, including customer-supplied eMTAs."
- **The list can change.** Xfinity says it "may revise the requirements at any time," including "the compatible eMTA makes, models, versions, and styles."
- **It stays at your address.** "An eMTA used with Xfinity Voice service can't be moved to another address and used without our approval."

## How do I check whether my modem works with Xfinity Voice?

Use My Device Info. Xfinity's minimum-requirements page (updated November 10, 2026) says: "If you're using your own modem for Xfinity Voice, you can verify that it works with your service by visiting My Device Info." Check before you buy, and again if you already own a voice modem from an earlier setup, because Xfinity can revise the compatible models.

The same page lists the rest of the setup:

1. **A phone that fits.** Handsets must comply with FCC Part 68, have a ringer equivalence number (REN) of no more than 1.0 and support touch-tone dialing.
2. **The right outlet.** The modem's outlet needs at least 15 amps of circuit capacity and can't be a switched outlet.
3. **Splitters if you need them.** You need one to connect more than one corded phone or wireless base to the modem.

Xfinity also keeps a list of phones "known to have a high probability of errors" with Xfinity Voice, including some AT&T, GE, Motorola and Uniden models and Bang & Olufsen BeoCom phones. If you're keeping an old handset, check that page.

### NETGEAR CM2050V: for plans faster than 1 Gbps

The CM2050V has "One (1) LAN 2.5Gbps Gigabit Ethernet port" and "Two (2) RJ-11 telephone ports." That 2.5 Gbps port is the reason to pick it: it's the only model here with an Ethernet port faster than gigabit, which matters if your plan is faster than 1 Gbps and your router has a matching port (our [multi-gig router guide](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) explains the port math). NETGEAR says it "Will not work with any other voice service except XFINITY Voice." The same page also calls it "Compatible with all major US Cable Service Providers," which contradicts that, so treat it as an Xfinity device.

### ARRIS SURFboard T25: for plans up to 800 Mbps

ARRIS rates the T25 as "best for Xfinity internet speed plans up to 800 Mbps," with "Two 1-Gigabit Ethernet Ports" and "Two Telephony ports for digital voice service." It has the longest warranty here, at two years. One caution: Best Buy's listing said on November 21, 2026, that it's "no longer available in new condition," so check stock before planning around it.

### NETGEAR CM1150V: an older model

The CM1150V's data sheet (copyright 2018) lists "Four (4) Gigabit Ethernet ports" and two phone lines, and says it "Requires XFINITY from Comcast Voice service plan." It advertises link aggregation for multi-gig speed, but a footnote says that feature "requires a firmware upgrade which is not currently available for this product." NETGEAR's current CM1150V web address shows the CM2050V's product page. If you already own one, check it in My Device Info; if you're buying, the CM2050V is NETGEAR's current page for Xfinity Voice.

NETGEAR's CM2050V page doesn't mention a backup battery, and these are modems, not Wi-Fi routers (ARRIS lists built-in Wi-Fi as "No" for the T25; NETGEAR says a wireless router is not included with the CM2050V). You'll need a separate router or mesh system; our [best mesh Wi-Fi for a large house](/guides/best-mesh-wifi-for-large-house/) guide covers bigger homes.

## What do I give up with my own voice modem?

- **Support certainty.** Xfinity doesn't guarantee compatibility with customer-supplied eMTAs, and remedies for an incompatibility are limited to those in its subscriber agreement.
- **Speed match.** Xfinity says your own modem "must work with Xfinity and support your internet speed."

Our [Xfinity modem guide](/guides/best-modem-for-xfinity/) covers data-only modems if you're dropping the phone line instead.

## Does Xfinity Voice work in a power outage?

Not without a battery. Xfinity says "your Xfinity Voice service will cease to operate during the power outage if you do not have a backup battery." It also warns that "A power outage in either the network or in your home could affect your ability to dial 911."

A battery backup "can be purchased for your Xfinity Voice modem," Xfinity says. With one installed, "only the primary phone number will work." Xfinity's battery page describes it as supporting service "for several hours" and says phones and alarm systems "should remain fully operational for up to 24 hours." Treat 24 hours as Xfinity's upper estimate, not a promise. Xfinity's battery page doesn't say whether its battery fits a customer-owned modem; the CM2050V page doesn't mention one, so ask Xfinity before relying on it.

If a home alarm uses the phone line, Xfinity says to "consult your home alarm company before having Xfinity Voice installed," and that a backup battery "may provide limited additional service" for the alarm, though that's not guaranteed.

## What about 911?

Xfinity says E911 sends your phone number and location to the 911 operator automatically. If you move, tell Comcast before you go: "If you have not informed Comcast of your new address, your 911 calls may be directed to the wrong emergency authorities."

## Bottom line

A voice modem of your own lets you keep Xfinity Voice without renting Xfinity's equipment, and the CM2050V (for multi-gig plans) and T25 (ARRIS says plans up to 800 Mbps) are two retail options their makers sell for it. Check the exact model in My Device Info first, plan for a backup battery if the phone matters in an outage, and remember Xfinity doesn't guarantee customer-owned eMTAs.
