For most homes, the **Orbi 770** is the one to buy: it has the same three Wi-Fi bands (2.4, 5 and 6 GHz) as the Orbi 870, a 2.5 Gig internet port, and it listed at $449.99 for a two-pack on NETGEAR.com on November 9, 2026. Pick the **Orbi 870** ($999.99 two-pack) only if your internet plan is faster than 2.5 Gbps or you want four 2.5 Gig Ethernet ports on every unit. The **Orbi 970** ($1,299.99 two-pack) is the only one with a separate Wi-Fi band reserved for traffic between the router and satellites, and the only one with a 10 Gbps Ethernet port on each satellite.

This guide compares NETGEAR's three Wi-Fi 7 Orbi series using NETGEAR's US product pages and prices. We have not tested these systems ourselves, so the speed classes and coverage figures below are NETGEAR's claims, not our measurements. Where NETGEAR's own pages contradict each other, we say so.

## What actually separates them

### Backhaul: the 970's extra band

In a mesh system, "backhaul" is the link between the router and its satellites. If that link shares radio time with your phones and laptops, both compete for the same airtime.

The Orbi 970 is a quad-band system. NETGEAR lists its bands as "6GHz, 5GHz-1, 5GHz-2, 2.4GHz," and its series page describes "Enhanced Dedicated Backhaul on the 5Ghz band." The product FAQ says dedicated backhaul "gives communication between the Orbi router and satellites its own band."

The 770 and 870 are tri-band. NETGEAR's 870 spec table lists "Backhaul: Shared 5 GHz & 6 GHz" and "Fronthaul: 5 GHz & 6 GHz," and both FAQs say the same thing: "A separate band used only for backhaul is exclusive to Orbi 970." Instead, the 770 and 870 use Wi-Fi 7 Multi-Link Operation (MLO); our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO explainer](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) covers what that does.

One thing to watch: the 770 page's description text says "Enhanced Dedicated Backhaul," and its spec table says "enhanced backhaul." Going by NETGEAR's own FAQ on the same page, that doesn't mean a separate band.

If you can run Ethernet between rooms, the backhaul difference shrinks. NETGEAR lists an Ethernet cable in the box of all three "for a wired backhaul option," and says every 870 satellite "has 2.5 Gig Ethernet for optional wired backhaul." Our [wired vs. wireless backhaul guide](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/) explains the setup.

### Ethernet ports set the speed ceiling

The internet port caps how much of your plan reaches the network. The 770's router has "One (1) 10/100/1000/2500 Mbps WAN port," so plans above 2.5 Gbps can't get through it at full speed. The 870 and 970 both have a 10 Gig internet port.

LAN ports matter if you have wired PCs, a NAS or a wired backhaul run:

- **Orbi 770:** three 2.5 Gbps LAN ports on the router, two on each satellite.
- **Orbi 870:** four 2.5 Gbps LAN ports on the router and on each satellite.
- **Orbi 970:** the router has one 10 Gbps LAN port and four 2.5 Gbps ports; each satellite has one 10 Gbps LAN port and two 2.5 Gbps ports.

These port counts come from NETGEAR's detailed spec sections. If one port matters to you, check the quick-start guide before buying.

If you're not sure your plan needs multi-gig at all, our [multi-gig router guide](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) walks through it.

### Speed classes aren't real-world speeds

BE11000, BE21000 and BE27000 add up the theoretical maximum link rates of every band on one unit. No single device gets that speed, and it says nothing about your internet plan. Treat the class as a rough ranking of radio capability, not a prediction of throughput.

### Coverage and device counts are NETGEAR's estimates

NETGEAR rates the 770 at up to 2,500 sq. ft. per unit, the 870 at 3,000 and the 970 at 3,300. These are best-case figures; walls, floors and layout change real coverage. The 770 page also lists the satellite at 2,750 sq. ft. in its pack selector, while its spec table says 2,500, and the 870 page lists both 100 and 150 devices. (The 870 page's hidden keywords also say BE27000; its visible specs say BE21000, which we use.) For layout-based advice, see our [best mesh Wi-Fi for a large house guide](/guides/best-mesh-wifi-for-large-house/).

## What all three share

- **Wi-Fi 7 with 6 GHz.** Every unit has a 6 GHz radio, which NETGEAR notes "is limited to indoor range." Your devices need 6 GHz support to use it; our [6 GHz support guide](/guides/check-6ghz-wifi-support/) shows how to check.
- **Optional Armor.** NETGEAR Armor, "powered by Bitdefender," is optional. All three product pages say "A free trial is included, and a paid subscription is optional after the trial."
- **Warranty conditions.** NETGEAR's pages show a one-year warranty. The 770 page says the limited warranty is "valid only if purchased from a NETGEAR authorized reseller," and the 870 page adds that you need to register the device.
- **Gigabit-plus plans need the right modem.** NETGEAR's footnote on the 770 page: "Gigabit service plans & compatible cable modem required for Gigabit or Multi-Gig Internet speeds."

## Bottom line

Start with the Orbi 770. It covers the same bands as the 870, has a 2.5 Gig internet port for plans up to 2.5 Gbps, and costs less than half as much per two-pack at NETGEAR.com today. Move to the 870 if your plan is faster than 2.5 Gbps or you want more multi-gig ports at each satellite. The 970 earns its price in a large, busy home where Ethernet backhaul isn't possible, because it's the only one that keeps router-to-satellite traffic on its own band.
