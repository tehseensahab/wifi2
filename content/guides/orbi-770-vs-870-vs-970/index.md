**Orbi 770 vs. 870 vs. 970 comes down to your internet plan and your wiring.** If your plan is 2.5 Gbps or slower, the **Orbi 770** is the one to buy: its router has 2.5 Gig ports, NETGEAR rates the 2-pack for up to 5,500 sq ft, and it cost $549.99 at netgear.com on November 8, 2026. The **Orbi 970** is for homes that will use 10 Gig LAN ports on the router and satellites, or want a fourth band reserved for the mesh link; its Black Edition 2-pack was $1,349.99 the same day. The **Orbi 870** is the middle option, with a 10 Gig WAN port and four 2.5 Gig ports on every unit, but NETGEAR's US store listed its 2-pack and 3-pack as out of stock.

This is a research-based comparison. We haven't tested these systems. Specs come from NETGEAR's US product pages and launch release, two US retailers and one third-party review; prices and stock were checked on November 8, 2026 and change often. If you're still deciding whether a NETGEAR system is right for a big home at all, start with our [best mesh Wi-Fi for a large house](/guides/best-mesh-wifi-for-large-house/) guide.

The "up to 11/21/27 Gbps" numbers add up the maximum link rates of every band. No single phone or laptop connects at those speeds, and real throughput is lower still. Treat them as a ranking of radio capability, not a speed you'll see on a speed test.

## Start with your internet plan

The WAN port is where your internet connection enters the system, and it's the clearest dividing line between these three.

- **Plans up to 2.5 Gbps:** the Orbi 770's 2.5 Gig WAN port can carry the whole plan. So can the other two, but you'd be paying for headroom you can't use on the internet side.
- **Plans above 2.5 Gbps:** the 770 caps you at 2.5 Gbps on the way in. The 870 and 970 both have a 10 Gig WAN port. The 870's LAN ports are all 2.5 Gig, though, so a single wired computer still tops out at 2.5 Gbps; only the 970 has a 10 Gig LAN port on the router.

NETGEAR's own launch release for the 870 lists its recommended internet speed as "Up to 2.5 Gig," even though it has a 10 Gig WAN port. Our [multi-gig router explainer](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) covers when ports faster than gigabit actually make a difference.

## Then look at your wiring

If you can run Ethernet between the router and satellites, the satellite ports matter as much as the router's. A wired link between units (wired backhaul) frees the mesh from relaying traffic over the air; our guide to [wired vs. wireless backhaul](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/) explains how to set it up.

- **Orbi 770:** each satellite has two 2.5 Gig ports, per Adorama's and B&H's listings. That's enough for wired backhaul at 2.5 Gbps plus one wired device.
- **Orbi 870:** each satellite has four 2.5 Gig ports, the most of the three, which helps if you have several wired devices in a room away from the router.
- **Orbi 970:** each satellite has one 10 Gig port and two 2.5 Gig ports. It's the only one with 10 Gig ports on both the router and the satellites, so it's the only one that can carry a 10 Gig wired link between units.

If you can't run cable, the Wi-Fi link between units does the work. The 970 lists a dedicated backhaul band among its four. Dong Knows Tech describes the 870 as "tri-band without a permanently dedicated backhaul" and judged its performance good; NETGEAR's pages don't say the 770 has a dedicated backhaul band either.

## Orbi 770: best value for plans up to 2.5 Gbps

**What NETGEAR lists:** tri-band Wi-Fi 7 "up to 11Gbps," a router with "2.5G WAN + 3x 2.5G LAN," up to 100 devices, and coverage of up to 5,500 sq ft for the 2-pack and 8,000 sq ft for the 3-pack. An add-on satellite adds up to 2,750 sq ft, per Adorama's listing.

**Price and stock (November 8, 2026):** $549.99 for the 2-pack (RBE772) at netgear.com. NETGEAR's series page listed the add-on satellite at $299.99; Adorama had it in stock at $349.99. B&H showed the 2-pack in stock but didn't display a price.

**Who it's for:** most homes on gigabit or 2 Gbps plans that want Wi-Fi 7 and 2.5 Gig ports without paying for 10 Gig hardware they won't use.

**What to know:** the internet port is the ceiling. If you expect to move to a plan above 2.5 Gbps while you own it, look at the 870 (if in stock) or the 970.

## Orbi 870: the middle option, if you can find it

**What NETGEAR lists:** "BE21000 Tri-Band WiFi 7," a router with "(1) 10 Gig internet WAN port" and four 2.5 Gig LAN ports, satellites with four 2.5 Gig ports each, a "Quad-core 1.5 GHz processor," and up to 3,000 sq ft per unit (6,000 sq ft for a 2-pack, 9,000 sq ft for a 3-pack). NETGEAR positioned it at launch for "Uncompromising Performance for Large homes."

**Price and stock (November 8, 2026):** NETGEAR's US Black Edition page showed "Limited Availability" and listed the 2-pack and 3-pack as out of stock. The add-on satellite was listed at $399.99, down from $549.99. At launch in January 2025, NETGEAR priced the 3-pack (RBE873) at $1,299.99 MSRP; that's a launch price, not a current one.

**Who it's for:** someone who finds a 2-pack or 3-pack in stock and wants a 10 Gig WAN port, or needs four wired ports in rooms away from the router.

**What to know:** there's no 10 Gig LAN port anywhere in the system. Dong Knows Tech called that "the Orbi 870's biggest shortcoming compared to the Orbi 970." NETGEAR's page also gives two different device limits (150 and 100). And because NETGEAR listed the packs as out of stock, check stock before planning around this one. We found no NETGEAR statement that the 870 has been discontinued.

## Orbi 970: for 10 Gig wiring and the most devices

**What NETGEAR lists:** "BE27000 WiFi 7 Performance," four bands (6 GHz, two 5 GHz bands and 2.4 GHz) with dedicated backhaul, a router with a 10 Gig WAN port, a 10 Gig LAN port and four 2.5 Gig LAN ports, satellites with a 10 Gig LAN port and two 2.5 Gig LAN ports, a quad-core 2.2 GHz processor, and up to 200 connected devices. Coverage is up to 3,300 sq ft for the router and 6,600 sq ft for a 2-pack.

**Price and stock (November 8, 2026):** $1,349.99 for the Black Edition 2-pack (RBE972SB) at netgear.com, listed down from $1,499.99. An add-on satellite (RBE970) was $799.99. NETGEAR's page didn't state stock; B&H showed the white 2-pack (RBE972S) in stock without a visible price.

**Who it's for:** homes with a plan above 2.5 Gbps, a 10 Gig NAS or PC, or Cat 6A wiring between rooms that they want to run at 10 Gbps. It's also the one to pick if you have a lot of devices: NETGEAR rates it for twice as many as the 770.

**What to know:** the Black Edition 2-pack costs $800 more than a 770 2-pack at NETGEAR's November 8 prices, and each extra satellite costs more than twice as much as a 770 satellite. One quirk: the satellite page lists coverage as up to 3,300 sq ft in its specs but says "Easily add 2,700 sq. ft." in its introduction.

## What all three share

- **Security subscription:** each includes a free trial of NETGEAR Armor, and NETGEAR says a paid subscription is optional after the trial. NETGEAR's pages don't state the trial length. Dong Knows Tech listed Armor at $99.99 a year in its January 2025 review; check the current price before you subscribe. If you're weighing subscription costs across brands, our [eero Plus explainer](/guides/is-eero-plus-worth-it/) covers eero's equivalent.
- **Warranty:** NETGEAR's 770 and 970 pages mention a one-year warranty; the 970 page says it applies only if you buy from an authorized reseller and register the device.
- **No mixing promise:** we didn't find a NETGEAR page saying 770, 870 and 970 units work together in one system. Buy satellites from the same series as your router.

## Which should you buy?

- **Plan of 2.5 Gbps or slower, no 10 Gig devices:** Orbi 770. It's the cheapest of the three, and its 2.5 Gig WAN port carries your whole plan.
- **Plan above 2.5 Gbps, but your wired devices top out at 2.5 Gbps:** Orbi 870 if you can find it in stock; otherwise the 970.
- **10 Gig NAS, 10 Gig PC, or 10 Gig wiring between floors:** Orbi 970.
- **Over 150 devices on the network:** Orbi 970, the only one NETGEAR rates for up to 200. (The 870's spec table says 150, though one line on its page says 100.)

Before you buy, compare against eero and TP-Link options in our [best mesh Wi-Fi for gigabit internet](/guides/best-mesh-wifi-for-gigabit-internet/) guide; on a 1 Gbps plan, a cheaper system may serve you just as well.
