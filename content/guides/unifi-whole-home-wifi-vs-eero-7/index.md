**Buy the eero 7 if you want the cheaper, simpler of the two; buy UniFi Whole Home WiFi if you want 6 GHz, a 10 GbE WAN port and a full web interface, and you're willing to pay about $75 to $80 more per node (about $94 to $99 with Ubiquiti's listed surcharge).** On November 15, 2026, eero.com listed an eero 7 3-pack at $319.99, while Ubiquiti's US store listed the Whole Home WiFi 3-pack at $559 and showed it as sold out. If you want 6 GHz but prefer eero's app, the eero Pro 7 is the closer match.

This comparison is based on eero's and Ubiquiti's product pages and announcements, plus one independent review. We haven't tested either system ourselves.

## The short version

| If you... | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Want the lowest price per node | eero 7 | $319.99 for 3 units vs. $559 for UniFi's 3-pack (Nov. 15, 2026) |
| Have phones and laptops that support 6 GHz | UniFi Whole Home WiFi, or eero Pro 7 | eero 7 has no 6 GHz radio |
| Have a 10 GbE modem or ONT port | UniFi Whole Home WiFi | 10 GbE WAN per Express 7; eero 7 has 2.5 GbE ports |
| Want app-based setup with little to configure | eero 7 | eero says "It's all in the app" |
| Want a full web interface | UniFi Whole Home WiFi | Web interface with an optional mobile app, per Dong Knows Tech |
| Need two wired devices at each extra node | eero 7 | Two 2.5 GbE ports per unit vs. one LAN port on the Express 7 (on the main unit, one port connects to your modem) |

## Hardware: dual-band vs. 6 GHz

**eero 7.** eero's [support page](https://eero.com/support/articles/eero-7) describes "dual-band 2.4 and 5 GHz antennas," 2x2 on each band, "wireless speeds up to 1.8 Gbps" and "Two 2.5GbE ports." It lists "up to 2,000 sq. ft. (190 m²) of indoor coverage per unit" and "Multi-Link operation (MLO) support." The device count varies by page: the support page says it "can support 120+ devices," while eero's [comparison page](https://eero.com/support/articles/eero-mesh-wifi-product-comparison) says "100+ devices."

**UniFi Express 7.** Whole Home WiFi is the Express 7 sold in multi-packs. In its [February 2025 launch post](https://blog.ui.com/article/introducing-all-new-10-gigabit-unifi-cloud-gateways), Ubiquiti lists "Integrated 6-stream WiFi 7 with 6 GHz support," a "(1) 10 GbE RJ45" WAN port, "(1) 2.5 GbE RJ45 LAN port to extend network," 300 clients and 2.3 Gbps of IDS/IPS throughput. Ubiquiti's Whole Home WiFi announcement doesn't give a coverage area.

What this means in practice:

- **6 GHz matters if your devices use it.** Without a 6 GHz radio, eero 7 runs Wi-Fi 7 on 2.4 and 5 GHz only. Our [dual-band Wi-Fi 7 guide](/guides/dual-band-wifi-7/) explains what you give up, and our [6 GHz support guide](/guides/check-6ghz-wifi-support/) shows how to check your phone or laptop.
- **Ports.** eero 7 gives you two 2.5 GbE ports on every unit; the Express 7 has one WAN and one LAN port. If you'll wire a TV, console and PC at one spot, you'll need a switch with either system, sooner with the Express 7.
- **Internet speed.** eero says the eero 7 is "Ideal for internet plans up to 2.5 Gbps." The Express 7's 10 GbE WAN only matters if your plan and modem go faster than that. Our [multi-gig guide](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) covers when it's worth it.

Speed figures here are maximum rates from the makers, not the speed you'll see on a device.

## Mesh backhaul

Ubiquiti's [Whole Home WiFi announcement](https://blog.ui.com/article/introducing-whole-home-wifi-by-unifi), published September 2, 2026, promises "True multi-gigabit wireless meshing with WiFi 7" and "Full-duplex multi-link operation." [Dong Knows Tech's review](https://dongknows.com/ux7-3-unifi-home-wi-fi-7-whole-home-wifi-mesh-review/) says the Express 7 "will use the bonded 5GHz+6GHz link as the backhaul when a network cable isn't an option," with "no 2.4GHz band in the backhaul MLO link."

eero doesn't say which band carries eero 7's wireless backhaul, but without a 6 GHz radio it can only use 2.4 or 5 GHz, the bands your devices also use. If you can run Ethernet between nodes, either system can use a wired link; Dong Knows Tech mentions 2.5GbE or 10GbE wired backhaul for UniFi, and eero 7 has 2.5 GbE ports. Our [wired vs. wireless backhaul guide](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/) explains the difference.

## Setup, apps and subscriptions

eero's shop page says "It's all in the app" and that you can "set up in minutes with the app." Ubiquiti says Whole Home WiFi has "No mandatory apps or complicated steps," and Dong Knows Tech describes a "comprehensive web user interface with an optional mobile app." If you want a full web interface, UniFi has one; if you want to set it and forget it, eero asks less of you.

On paid extras:

- **eero:** "Add an eero Plus subscription for Advanced Security, Ad Blocking, Parental Controls, Malwarebytes, 1Password, VPN powered by Guardian, and more," eero's shop page says; the same page lists a "Free eero app with parental controls." Our [eero Plus guide](/guides/is-eero-plus-worth-it/) covers the price and whether it's worth it.
- **UniFi:** The Express 7 store page lists optional add-ons, including "Enhanced Cyber Security" at "$99.00/unit," "Billed Annually"; "UI Care" ("5 Year Coverage") at "$80.00/unit"; and a "UniFi 5G ISP Failover Kit" at $119.

## Which should you buy?

- **eero 7:** You want Wi-Fi 7 mesh for the least money, your internet plan is 2.5 Gbps or less, and you'd rather manage everything from a phone app. It's also the easier pick if you need two wired ports at each node.
- **UniFi Whole Home WiFi:** Your devices support 6 GHz, you want a 6 GHz wireless backhaul, your modem or ONT has a 10 GbE port, or you want UniFi's full web interface. Check stock first: the 3-pack was sold out on November 15, 2026.
- **eero Pro 7:** You want 6 GHz but prefer eero's app and its 5 GbE ports; eero lists it at 3.9 Gbps wireless and up to 2,500 sq ft per device on its comparison page. Our [eero 7 vs. Pro 7 vs. Max 7 guide](/guides/eero-7-vs-eero-pro-7-vs-eero-max-7/) compares the eero lineup.
