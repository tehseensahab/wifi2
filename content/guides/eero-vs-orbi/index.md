**Choose eero if you want an app-only, simple mesh system and a 3-year warranty (NETGEAR lists 1 year on the Orbi 870); choose NETGEAR Orbi if you want a browser interface, bigger coverage claims per pack or a quad-band top model.** eero runs entirely from a phone app and lists a 3-year limited warranty on its Wi-Fi 7 models. Orbi can also be managed from a web browser, and NETGEAR rates its packs for more square footage. Prices differ most at entry level: on November 10, 2026, an eero 7 3-pack was $399.99 on eero.com and an Orbi 770 3-pack was $599.99 on netgear.com (on sale from $699.99), but the eero 7 is dual-band, while the Orbi 770 adds a third, 6 GHz band.

This is a research-based comparison; we haven't tested these systems. Specs, prices and policies come from eero's and NETGEAR's websites (US pages, prices in dollars) and support pages, checked on November 10, 2026. Prices change often. For model-by-model detail within each brand, see our [eero 7 vs. Pro 7 vs. Max 7](/guides/eero-7-vs-eero-pro-7-vs-eero-max-7/) and [Orbi 770 vs. 870 vs. 970](/guides/orbi-770-vs-870-vs-970/) guides.

## The short answer by reader

- **You want set-and-forget, and you're happy with a phone app:** eero.
- **You want to log in from a laptop and dig through settings:** Orbi.
- **Your budget is tight, and a dual-band system is enough:** eero 7.
- **You want 6 GHz at the lowest Orbi price:** Orbi 770.
- **You have a multi-gig plan and want 10 Gig ports on the router and satellites:** Orbi 970. If you prefer eero's app, the eero Max 7 is its top model (four Ethernet ports, wired speeds up to 9.4 Gbps).
- **You care most about warranty length:** eero (3 years on its Wi-Fi 7 line, by eero's warranty page).

## How the Wi-Fi 7 lineups line up

Both brands sell three main indoor Wi-Fi 7 tiers. They don't map perfectly, so this table pairs them by role, not by identical specs.

| Tier | eero model | Orbi model |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | **eero 7**: dual-band, two 2.5 GbE ports per unit, up to 2,000 sq ft per unit, 100+ devices | **Orbi 770**: tri-band (2.4, 5 and 6 GHz); router has one 2.5 Gbps WAN and three 2.5 Gbps LAN ports, each satellite two 2.5 Gbps ports; 3-pack up to 8,000 sq. ft.; up to 100 devices |
| Middle | **eero Pro 7**: tri-band, two 5 GbE ports per unit, 200+ devices | **Orbi 870**: tri-band; router has a 10 Gig WAN and four 2.5 Gbps LAN ports, each satellite four 2.5 Gbps ports; 3-pack up to 9,000 sq. ft. |
| Top | **eero Max 7**: tri-band, four Ethernet ports, up to 2,500 sq ft per unit, 200+ devices | **Orbi 970**: quad-band (2.4 GHz, two 5 GHz, 6 GHz); router has a 10 Gig WAN, a 10 Gbps LAN and four 2.5 Gbps LAN ports; satellite has one 10 Gbps and two 2.5 Gbps ports; 2-pack up to 6,600 sq. ft.; up to 200 devices |

A few cautions on these numbers. eero's own pages disagree on the Pro 7's coverage: its shop page says 2,000 sq. ft. per unit, while its product comparison says up to 2,500. NETGEAR's Orbi 870 page lists both 100 and 150 devices in different places. And all coverage and device figures are maker claims, not measurements.

The speed ratings aren't comparable either. eero says its maximum speeds "reflect combined supported speeds across wired and wireless clients" and that maximum wireless rates "are derived from IEEE 802.11 standard." NETGEAR's "up to 11 Gbps," "21 Gbps" and "27 Gbps" figures for the 770, 870 and 970 are also combined "up to" ratings for each system's bands. Neither is a speed one laptop or phone should expect.

## App vs. browser: the biggest everyday difference

eero is app-only. Its support page says, "Your eero network can only be managed using the mobile app," and that it "cannot be configured through a program or web browser using Windows, Linux, or Intel-based Mac machines." The one exception: owners of Apple Silicon Macs can install the iPad version of the eero app.

Orbi has an app too, but NETGEAR's help article says, "You can also access your Orbi network by typing orbilogin.net into your browser when locally connected to your Orbi network." That article doesn't list which Orbi models it applies to, so check your model's manual if browser access matters to you.

For most households, the app is all you'll use. Browser access matters if you'd rather change settings like port forwarding or DNS from a laptop, or if you don't want your network tied to a phone.

## Warranty and subscriptions

**Warranty.** eero lists a "3 Year Limited Warranty" for the eero 7, Pro 7 and Max 7 (along with its PoE 7 and Outdoor 7), for units "purchased from eero.com and our official retail partners." NETGEAR's Orbi 870 page lists a "1-year warranty," and its Orbi 770 page says the limited warranty is "valid only if purchased from a NETGEAR authorized reseller."

**Subscriptions.** Both are optional.

- **eero Plus:** $9.99/month (one-month free trial) or $99.99/year (two-month free trial). eero's page lists content filters, advanced Wi-Fi scheduling, ad blocking, active threat protection, a VPN and password management among its features. Our [eero Plus guide](/guides/is-eero-plus-worth-it/) covers whether it's worth paying for.
- **NETGEAR Armor:** $99.99/year; **Armor Plus:** $149.99/year, each covering up to 50 devices on NETGEAR's purchase page. NETGEAR's help article says you can try Armor free for 30 days and that the annual price "depends on the region in which you live."

Over three years, eero Plus or basic Armor adds about $300 at the annual rates above (Armor Plus about $450), which is more than the price gap between the two brands at the entry tier.

## Mixing and upgrading

eero says, "All eeros are compatible with each other no matter when they were released," which makes adding a unit later easy. It warns that mixing an eero Pro 6E with older-generation eeros "may result in reduced performance." NETGEAR's pages we checked don't document mixing Orbi generations, and neither brand documents mixing with the other's mesh units. Plan to stay with one brand.

## Which should you buy?

- **Buy eero 7** if you want Wi-Fi 7 mesh at the lowest price, your plan is 2.5 Gbps or slower and a dual-band system is enough.
- **Buy eero Pro 7** if you want eero's app and warranty with a third (tri-) band and faster 5 GbE ports.
- **Buy Orbi 770** if you want 6 GHz for less than an eero Pro 7 3-pack, plus browser access.
- **Buy Orbi 970 (10 Gig ports) or eero Max 7 (wired speeds up to 9.4 Gbps)** only if you have a multi-gig plan and fast wired devices. The Orbi 970 is the only quad-band system here.
