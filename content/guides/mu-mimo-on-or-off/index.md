**Leave MU-MIMO on.** NETGEAR's support article says, "We recommend enabling MU-MIMO unless you experience connection issues with it turned on," and ASUS says the feature is "enabled by default" on its routers. The one risk NETGEAR names is that MU-MIMO "can in rare cases cause connection instability for older devices." So the only good reason to turn it off is to test whether it is behind an older device that keeps dropping off your Wi-Fi.

This guide is based on NETGEAR's and ASUS's support pages and Wi-Fi Alliance announcements. We have not tested MU-MIMO on specific routers.

## What does MU-MIMO do?

MU-MIMO stands for multi-user, multiple-input, multiple-output. Without it, a router sends data to one device at a time and switches between them very quickly. With it, NETGEAR says, MU-MIMO "allows an WiFi router or access point to transmit to multiple client devices simultaneously."

It is a capacity feature, not an internet-speed feature. It helps when several devices are pulling data at once, such as phones, laptops and streaming boxes in a busy house. NETGEAR is explicit about the limit: "While MU-MIMO doesn't increase the internet speed provided by your ISP, it optimizes the internal network."

ASUS describes it in terms of its four-stream routers: the feature "allocates segments of bandwidth to up to four separate devices. (4x4)". Treat that as ASUS's description of its 4x4 models, not a fixed number for every router.

## Should you turn MU-MIMO on or off?

| Your situation | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi works normally | **On** | NETGEAR recommends it; ASUS enables it by default |
| Lots of devices in use at once | **On** | That is the case MU-MIMO is built for |
| One older device keeps disconnecting | **Test off** | NETGEAR says it can rarely cause instability for older devices |
| Turning it off didn't fix the problem | **Back on** | The cause is elsewhere; see our guide to [why Wi-Fi keeps disconnecting](/guides/wifi-keeps-disconnecting/) |
| You want a faster internet plan speed | **No effect** | It doesn't change the speed your ISP delivers |

Change one setting at a time and give the network a day or so before judging. If you toggle MU-MIMO, [band steering](/guides/what-is-band-steering/) and [Wi-Fi 7 mode](/guides/wifi-7-mode-on-or-off/) together, you won't know which one made the difference.

## Do your devices need to support MU-MIMO?

Yes. NETGEAR's FAQ says "both the router and the client devices must support MU-MIMO to take full advantage of the router technology." ASUS says the feature works "automatically when compatible devices are detected/in use."

A device that doesn't support MU-MIMO can still connect; it just doesn't get the simultaneous-transmission benefit. Apart from the rare older-device instability NETGEAR mentions, leaving the feature on has little downside for most homes.

## MU-MIMO in Wi-Fi 5 vs. Wi-Fi 6 and later

The feature has changed across Wi-Fi generations:

- **Wi-Fi 5 (802.11ac Wave 2):** NETGEAR says MU-MIMO was "introduced midway through the WiFi 5 generation as part of the IEEE 802.11ac Wave 2 standard" and "operates in the downstream direction (broadcaster to client)."
- **Wi-Fi 6 at launch (2019):** Wi-Fi Alliance's Sept. 16, 2019 announcement listed MU-MIMO as a Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 6 feature that "allows more downlink data to be transferred at once."
- **Wi-Fi 6 Release 2 (2022):** Wi-Fi Alliance announced on Jan. 5, 2022 that "Wi-Fi CERTIFIED 6 Release 2 adds support for uplink multi-user MIMO, enabling devices to upload content concurrently to an access point (AP)."

NETGEAR's own explainer describes MU-MIMO in Wi-Fi 6, 6E and 7 networks as working "for both uplink and downlink wireless communications." Because uplink support came with a later certification release, check a specific device's spec sheet rather than assuming every Wi-Fi 6 product supports uplink MU-MIMO.

## How to turn MU-MIMO on or off

### NETGEAR routers

NETGEAR's steps (its article lists Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E models and XR1000 gaming routers):

1. Open a browser on a device connected to the router and go to routerlogin.net, then sign in with the admin user name and your password.
2. Select **ADVANCED > Advanced Setup > Wireless Settings**. NETGEAR notes some models use labels like **Settings > Advanced Settings > Wireless Setup**.
3. Select or clear the **Enable MU-MIMO** check box.
4. Click **Apply**.

Whether it's on out of the box depends on the router. NETGEAR's support article says MU-MIMO "might or might not be enabled by default" depending on the router's age and firmware, while its explainer says it is enabled by default "on the latest NETGEAR Devices." Check the box rather than assuming.

### ASUS routers

ASUS says MU-MIMO is "enabled by default and will be working automatically when compatible devices are detected/in use." Its FAQ groups models by support: some do MU-MIMO only on 5 GHz, some on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, and models with 6 GHz on 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz and 6 GHz. ASUS points to each model's spec page for details.

### Other brands

If your router's app or settings page doesn't show a MU-MIMO option, there may be no user setting to change. Check the maker's spec page or support documents to see whether the hardware supports it. If you're shopping for a router for a busy, multi-device home, our [best Wi-Fi router for a large house](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/) guide covers current picks.

## Bottom line

Keep MU-MIMO on. It helps when several compatible devices are busy at once, and devices that don't support it simply don't get the benefit. Turn it off only to test whether an older device's dropouts stop, and turn it back on if they don't.
