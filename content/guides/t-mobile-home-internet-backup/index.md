**T-Mobile Home Internet Backup is a second, 5G internet line for when your main cable or fiber connection goes down.** On November 20, 2026, T-Mobile listed it at $20 a month with AutoPay, plus taxes and fees, for 100 hours of unlimited data each billing cycle (its support page says the hours "reset each month"); after that, speeds drop to up to 600 kbps until the plan resets. It can switch over automatically, but T-Mobile's setup steps cover only devices you connect to its own Wi-Fi network, and the gateway needs power, so it won't help in a blackout without a battery.

This explainer is built from T-Mobile's plan page, support pages and press releases, checked on November 20, 2026. It is research-based: we have not used the service ourselves, and T-Mobile marks its bill credit and test-drive offers "Limited-time; subject to change," so check the price at your address before signing up.

## What does T-Mobile Home Internet Backup cost?

| Item | What T-Mobile lists |
|---|---|
| Monthly price | "$20/month with AutoPay, plus taxes and fees"; AutoPay discount needs a debit card, bank account or T-Mobile Visa, "otherwise $10 more/line/mo." |
| Voice line needed? | "No. A T-Mobile voice line is not required." |
| Optional discount | "$10 monthly bill credit" with a "postpaid voice line, and new Home Internet Backup line," limit one per account |
| Upfront | "$35 device connection charge due at sale" |
| Equipment | "No monthly equipment fees"; gateway included. "Return gateway undamaged or pay up to $370." |
| Data | "100 hours of unlimited data" each billing cycle |
| After 100 hours | "speeds up to 600Kbps" until the plan resets |
| Extra hours | Three 100-hour Backup passes per year "at no extra charge" |

T-Mobile says the bill credit stops "if you cancel any lines or change plans." It also lists "Credit approval required," says the plan is "Not available in all areas," and restricts service "away from approved address." T-Mobile also advertises a 15-day test drive for qualifying new Home Internet lines; check that it applies to a Backup line before relying on it.

At $20 for 100 hours, the plan works out to 20 cents per hour of backup if you use all of it ($20 ÷ 100 hours). If you never have an outage, you're paying $20 a month for standby.

## Who is it for?

T-Mobile says Backup is meant to "keep cable or fiber customers connected during outages from their primary internet service provider," and is for "T-Mobile Fiber customers or people with an existing primary internet provider." If you want T-Mobile 5G as your main connection, that's the regular Home Internet service; our [T-Mobile Home Internet plans guide](/guides/t-mobile-home-internet-plans/) compares those plans.

## How does the 100-hour limit work?

T-Mobile meters Backup in time, not data. Its support page says, "Time only counts when Backup is active. 100 hours reset each month and do not roll over," and you can "Pause Backup Wi-Fi to turn it off and stop depleting time." The plan page says usage counts "while you're connected to T-Mobile Backup Wi-Fi, not full calendar days."

Within those hours, data is unlimited. Once the hours are gone, the plan slows to "up to 600 kbps until your plan resets." In our judgment, that's enough for messages and email but not for video calls or streaming.

For longer outages, T-Mobile gives "three 100-hour Backup passes per year at no extra charge." Each pass "expires 30 days after adding it," so add one only when you need it.

**This has changed before.** When T-Mobile launched Backup on June 6, 2024, it cost "$30 a month with AutoPay" (or $20 for a limited time with an eligible voice line) and included "130 GB of 5G data a month." In November 2024 T-Mobile added three free 130 GB passes a year. Droid Life reported on March 30, 2026 that "T-Mobile has switched from giving you 130GB to 100 hours each month." Old reviews quoting 130 GB are out of date.

## How does automatic failover work?

T-Mobile offers two modes on its gateway support page:

**Automatic.** "A Gen4 Gateway and ethernet cable is required for automatic connection." The steps:

1. "Place your T-Mobile gateway near your primary router."
2. "Use an ethernet cable to link your primary router to the LAN1 port on your T-Mobile gateway."
3. Make sure the Backup gateway's Wi-Fi name (SSID) is different from your main network's.
4. "Link any devices that should use Backup to T-Mobile Wi-Fi and enable auto-reconnect for each one."
5. Set up and manage the plan in the T-Life app. T-Mobile says, "You must use T-Life to set up and manage your Backup Plan to have optimal speeds."

T-Mobile's explainer says a compatible gateway "can detect when your primary ISP goes down and switch to the backup connection automatically," and "When the outage is resolved, it switches back."

**Manual.** "You can use this mode with any gateway." When your main internet goes down, you move devices to the T-Mobile Wi-Fi yourself, then back when it returns.

**The catch with both modes:** failover covers the devices joined to the T-Mobile Backup Wi-Fi. T-Mobile's steps don't describe switching devices that stay on your own router's Wi-Fi, such as a mesh system's network. If you want every device on your main network to fail over without moving them, that's what a [dual WAN router](/guides/best-dual-wan-router/) does, with a cellular or other backup line on its second WAN port. T-Mobile's pages don't say whether the Backup gateway can be used that way, so we can't confirm it.

## Does it work in a power outage?

Only with backup power. T-Mobile says the gateway "needs power to work" and covers outages only "if the gateway is plugged into a battery backup." Our guide to [whether internet works in a power outage](/guides/internet-power-outage/) explains what else needs power and how a battery backup (UPS) fits in.

## Will it be slower than regular T-Mobile Home Internet?

It can be at busy times. T-Mobile's plan terms say, "During congestion, customers on this plan may notice speeds lower than other customers and further reduction if using >600GB/mo., due to data prioritization." T-Mobile doesn't publish a typical speed range for Backup on the plan page we checked.

## Is T-Mobile Backup worth it?

It's worth considering if outages from your main provider cost you work time, and if T-Mobile's 5G signal is good where you live (if the 15-day test drive applies to your line, it's the cheapest way to find out). It's less attractive if:

- **Your outages are mostly power cuts.** You'll need a battery for the gateway regardless.
- **You want hands-off failover for every device.** Backup only covers devices on its own Wi-Fi.
- **You already have a phone plan with hotspot data.** Our [phone backup internet guide](/guides/phone-backup-internet-router/) covers using a phone as backup for your router.
- **You use eero.** eero sells its own cellular backup, eero Signal; our [eero Signal 4G vs. 5G guide](/guides/eero-signal-4g-vs-5g/) covers its data limits.
