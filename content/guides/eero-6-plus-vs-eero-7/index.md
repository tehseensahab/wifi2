**Buy the eero 6+ if your internet plan is 1 Gbps or slower; buy the eero 7 if you pay for more than a gigabit or want more coverage per unit.** The deciding spec is the Ethernet ports: eero says the eero 6+ "Supports wired or wireless speeds up to 940 Mbps" through two 1 GbE ports, while the eero 7 has "two auto-sensing 2.5 GbE ports" and "delivers wired speeds up to 2.3 Gbps." On Nov. 24, 2026, eero.com listed the eero 6+ 1-pack at $94.99 and the eero 7 1-pack at $139.99, both Black Friday sale prices.

This is a research-based comparison built from eero's own spec pages and store. We have not tested either system.

Speed ratings are eero's figures, not measured throughput; what you see on a device depends on the device, distance and your internet plan.

## Which should you buy?

### Choose the eero 6+ if...

- **Your plan is 1 Gbps or slower.** eero's shop page calls it "Ideal for internet plans up to 1 Gbps," though eero rates it at up to 940 Mbps, so on a full 1 Gbps plan expect a little less than the advertised plan speed.
- **Price matters most.** At the Nov. 24 sale prices, a single eero 6+ cost $45 less than a single eero 7.
- **You're filling gaps in a smaller home.** One unit covers up to 1,500 sq ft by eero's rating, and eero says an eero 6+ with two eero 6+ extenders covers "up to 4,500 sq. ft."

### Choose the eero 7 if...

- **You pay for more than a gigabit, or plan to.** eero says the eero 7 is "well suited for 2.5 Gbps internet plans." eero rates the eero 6+ at up to 940 Mbps through its 1 GbE ports.
- **You want to wire things in at multi-gig speeds.** The 2.5 GbE ports matter for a wired backhaul between units or a fast desktop or NAS; see our guide to [wired vs. wireless backhaul](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/).
- **You need more coverage per unit.** eero rates it at 2,000 sq ft per unit, 500 sq ft more than the eero 6+. In a bigger home that can mean one fewer unit; our [best mesh Wi-Fi for a large house](/guides/best-mesh-wifi-for-large-house/) guide covers layouts in more detail.
- **You want a longer warranty.** eero's store lists a "3-year warranty" for the eero 7; the eero 6+ is "backed by a limited 1-year warranty," according to its support article.

### Consider a step up instead if...

You want the 6 GHz band. Neither model has it: both are dual-band. eero's higher-end Wi-Fi 7 models are compared in [eero 7 vs. eero Pro 7 vs. eero Max 7](/guides/eero-7-vs-eero-pro-7-vs-eero-max-7/).

## What Wi-Fi 7 actually adds on the eero 7

Because the eero 7 has no 6 GHz radio, its Wi-Fi 7 gains come from the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands. eero lists support for channels up to 240 MHz (the eero 6+ tops out at 160 MHz) and says "A new feature of the eero 7 is Multi-Link operation (MLO) support." Wider channels and MLO only help devices that support Wi-Fi 7; older phones and laptops connect the same way they would to a Wi-Fi 6 router. Our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO guide](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) explains when MLO matters.

For most homes, the more practical upgrade is the wired side: 2.5 GbE ports and a 2.3 Gbps wired rating instead of 940 Mbps.

## Can you mix eero 6+ and eero 7?

Yes. eero says, "All eeros are compatible with each other no matter when they were released," and "Once your gateway eero is installed, you may use any combination of eeros to set up your network." Both the eero 6+ and eero 7 are on eero's list of models that can be the gateway.

If you already own eero 6+ units and are moving to a plan faster than 1 Gbps, the logical upgrade is an eero 7 as the gateway at the modem, because that unit's 1 GbE port is what would otherwise cap the connection. eero doesn't publish figures for mixed networks, so we can't say how much speed you'd gain at the other units.

## Conflicting eero figures

eero's own pages disagree on device counts. The eero 6+ support article says "75+ simultaneous connected devices" while its shop page says "250+ devices." The eero 7 support article and shop page say 120+ devices, but eero's product comparison chart says "100+ devices." We haven't used device counts to make a recommendation for that reason.

## Bottom line

On a plan of 1 Gbps or less, the eero 6+ does the job for less money. If you pay for more than a gigabit, want multi-gig wired connections, or need fewer units to cover a larger home, the eero 7 is the better buy. If you also want 6 GHz, look at eero's tri-band models instead.
