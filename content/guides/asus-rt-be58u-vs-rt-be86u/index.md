**The ASUS RT-BE58U and RT-BE86U are both dual-band Wi-Fi 7 routers without a 6 GHz band, but they sit at different levels. The RT-BE58U is the entry model, with 2x2 radios, one 2.5G port and four gigabit ports. The RT-BE86U adds a 4x4 5 GHz radio, a 10G port, four 2.5G ports and a faster processor.** Choose the BE58U if your plan is about 2 Gbps or slower and your wired devices are fine at gigabit; choose the BE86U for faster plans or multi-gig wired devices.

This comparison is based on ASUS's published specifications, checked on November 28, 2026. We have not tested either router. The RT-BE58U figures come from ASUS's Canadian tech spec page and global product page, because ASUS's US spec page for that model didn't come up in our searches (the RT-BE86U figures come from ASUS's global spec page and US product page); B&H's US listing matches them on processor, memory, total rated speed and port count.

## Wi-Fi: same bands, twice the 5 GHz streams on the BE86U

Both routers use 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz, support channels up to 160 MHz and support Wi-Fi 7's Multi-Link Operation (MLO) across those two bands. ASUS's US page lists STR and EMLSR MLO modes for the RT-BE86U; for the RT-BE58U, ASUS says "Supported MLO modes and bands may vary by device." Our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO guide](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) explains what those modes mean.

The difference is radio size. The RT-BE86U has four streams on 5 GHz to the RT-BE58U's two, which doubles the maximum 5 GHz link rate on paper (5,764 vs. 2,882 Mbps). A phone or laptop links using its own number of antennas, so a single device won't see the router's full figure; the extra streams may help more when many devices are active at once. ASUS also claims more coverage for the BE86U (2,750 vs. 2,000 sq. ft.); those are ASUS's figures, not our measurements.

Neither router has 6 GHz, so neither can use Wi-Fi 7's 320 MHz channels. If your neighborhood's 5 GHz band is crowded and your devices support 6 GHz, read [Dual-Band Wi-Fi 7: What You Lose Without 6 GHz](/guides/dual-band-wifi-7/) first. For a large home, a mesh system is often the better answer than either router alone; see our [Wi-Fi router for a large house guide](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/).

## Ports: the deciding difference for most buyers

- **RT-BE58U: five Ethernet ports, one of them multi-gig.** ASUS lists one 2.5G WAN/LAN port, one 1G WAN/LAN port and three 1G LAN ports. If the 2.5G port connects to your modem or ONT, every wired device in the house gets at most 1 Gbps.
- **RT-BE86U: five Ethernet ports, all multi-gig.** One 10G WAN/LAN port, one 2.5G WAN/LAN port and three 2.5G LAN ports. ASUS sets one 2.5G LAN port up as a dedicated gaming port.

What that means in practice:

- **Internet plans up to about 2 Gbps:** the RT-BE58U's 2.5G WAN port can take the connection. With the 2.5G port on your modem, speeds above 1 Gbps reach your devices only over Wi-Fi, because the remaining wired ports are gigabit.
- **Plans above 2.5 Gbps:** only the RT-BE86U's 10G port can use them.
- **Multi-gig wired devices** (a NAS, a desktop with a 2.5G adapter, a wired mesh node or access point): the BE86U's 2.5G LAN ports run them at up to 2.5 Gbps without a separate switch. A 10G device would need the 10G port, or be capped at 2.5 Gbps on the others.

Our guide to [multi-gig routers and 2.5G and 10G ports](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) covers when faster ports pay off.

## Processor, memory and software

Both have 1 GB of RAM and 256 MB of flash; the BE86U's quad-core processor runs at 2.6 GHz to the BE58U's 2.0 GHz. ASUS doesn't publish a performance figure tied to that clock difference. It quotes the same VPN speeds over Ethernet for both routers: up to 300 Mbps with WireGuard and up to 150 Mbps with OpenVPN.

The software lists are close. Both support AiMesh as a primary router or a node, WireGuard and OpenVPN VPN clients and servers, Instant Guard and USB mobile tethering, and ASUS's spec pages list VPN Fusion for both. The security naming differs: the RT-BE58U spec sheet lists "AiProtection" and the RT-BE86U lists "AiProtection Pro," but both name the same four features (Router Security Assessment, Malicious Site Blocking, Two-Way IPS, and Infected Device Prevention and Blocking). ASUS's spec sheets don't explain what Pro adds on these two models, so we wouldn't pick between them on that basis. ASUS also markets the RT-BE58U for smart homes, with Smart Home Master for creating separate networks (SSIDs) and parental controls "all without subscriptions."

One detail to check if you buy in Canada: ASUS's Canadian store lists two RT-BE58U part numbers (90IG0A70-MX1C00 and 90IG0910-MX1C00) with nearly identical spec lists; they differ only in beamforming lines and whether VPN Fusion is listed. We found nothing from ASUS explaining the difference.

## Which should you buy?

- **Choose the RT-BE58U** if your internet plan is 2 Gbps or slower, your wired devices are fine at gigabit, and you want ASUS's Wi-Fi 7 features (MLO, AiMesh) in a lighter router (461 g vs. 854 g) for an apartment or a smaller home. Our [router for an apartment guide](/guides/best-router-for-apartment/) has other options in that space.
- **Choose the RT-BE86U** if you have, or expect, a plan faster than about 2 Gbps, own multi-gig wired devices, or want the 4x4 5 GHz radio for a larger or busier home. If you need even more wired ports, compare it with the RT-BE88U in our [RT-BE86U vs RT-BE88U guide](/guides/asus-rt-be86u-vs-rt-be88u/).
- **Choose neither** if most of your devices support 6 GHz and your 5 GHz band is congested; a Wi-Fi 7 router with a 6 GHz band can use it and these two can't.

We haven't listed prices: B&H listed the RT-BE58U as in stock but didn't show a price in the text we could read, and retail prices move often around holiday sales. Compare current prices when you buy; the gap between the two is what decides whether the BE86U's multi-gig ports are worth it for you.
