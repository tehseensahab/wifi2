The ARRIS SURFboard S33 and S34 are the same modem for downloads and different for uploads. Both have one 2.5 Gbps and one 1 Gbps Ethernet port, the same DOCSIS 3.1 channel counts and a rating for cable plans up to 2.5 Gbps. The S34 adds what SURFboard calls "Next Generation Upload Speed Optimized" support, with a switchable upstream filter designed for cable companies' faster upload tiers. **Buy the S34 if your cable provider has upgraded upload speeds in your area, or if you want the option later. Buy the S33 only if your area hasn't been upgraded and the S33 costs meaningfully less.**

This comparison is based on SURFboard's product pages, the S34 data sheet and launch announcement, and Xfinity's support documentation. We have not tested either modem.

## SURFboard S33 vs. S34: specs side by side

| | SURFboard S33 | SURFboard S34 |
|---|---|---|
| Type | DOCSIS 3.1 cable modem (no Wi-Fi) | DOCSIS 3.1 cable modem (no Wi-Fi) |
| Ethernet ports | One 2.5 Gbps, one 1 Gbps | One 2.5 Gbps, one 1 Gbps |
| DOCSIS 3.0 channels | 32 down x 8 up | 32 down x 8 up |
| DOCSIS 3.1 channels | 2 down x 2 up | 2 down x 2 up |
| Plans it's rated for | Up to 2.5 Gbps | Up to 2.5 Gbps |
| Next-generation upload | Not claimed on SURFboard's S33 pages | "Next Generation Upload Speed Optimized" |
| Upstream filter | Not stated on SURFboard's S33 pages | Switchable: 5-42 MHz or 5-85 MHz |
| Providers named | Cox, Spectrum, Xfinity "and other major US cable internet providers" | Same |
| Warranty | 2-year limited | 2-year limited |
| Launch price | Not found in an official source | $219 MSRP (Sept. 5, 2024) |

Both product pages describe the modems as "best for cable internet speed plans up to 2.5 Gbps." The 2.5 Gbps Ethernet port is what lets a plan faster than 1 Gbps reach your router, provided the router also has a 2.5 Gbps WAN port. SURFboard's pages don't describe combining the two ports. To see how DOCSIS 3.1 compares with older and newer modems, read our [DOCSIS 3.0 vs. 3.1 vs. 4.0 guide](/guides/docsis-3-0-vs-3-1-vs-4-0-cable-modem/).

## What does "next generation upload" mean on the S34?

It means the S34 can switch the part of the cable spectrum it uses for upload. A cable line splits its frequencies between download and upload. The S34 data sheet lists a "Switchable Diplexer US 5-42MHz/5-85MHz," where US means upstream: the modem can send on the traditional 5 to 42 MHz range or on a wider 5 to 85 MHz range. More upstream spectrum gives the cable company room for faster upload tiers. The S34 launch release describes this as support for "next-generation upload speed service tiers with switchable low to mid support."

SURFboard's S33 product and store pages don't mention next-generation upload or a switchable upstream filter. We could not find an S33 data sheet from SURFboard or a provider that lists its upstream frequency range, so we can't say whether the hardware could do it; what matters for buyers is that SURFboard markets only the S34 for these tiers.

## Do I need the S34 for Xfinity?

Only if you want the faster upload speeds in an area Xfinity has upgraded. Xfinity says the new upload speeds in its enhanced markets "are up to 5–10 times faster than before, ranging from 75–200 Mbps," and that "there are limited retail modems, eMTAs or gateways available in the marketplace" that support them. If your own modem isn't certified for the enhanced speeds, Xfinity says it "should work for service tier speeds available before the recent upgrade." Unless Xfinity lists the S33 as certified for your area's enhanced speeds, it should keep working, but at the older, slower upload.

There is a ceiling for both models. Xfinity's X-Class plans (X-300, X-500, X-Gig and X-2Gig) offer "equally fast upload and download speeds above 200 Mbps" in limited areas, and Xfinity says "customer-owned modems are not currently compatible with X-Class speeds." If you want X-Class, you'll need Xfinity's own leased equipment, according to Xfinity, not an S33 or S34.

## Which should you buy?

| Your situation | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Your cable provider has upgraded upload in your area, or lists faster upload tiers you want | S34 | At Xfinity, the faster tiers need a modem certified for them; SURFboard markets only the S34 for these tiers |
| You may move, or you expect your area to be upgraded | S34 | Same download hardware, plus the upload option |
| Your area hasn't been upgraded, you don't plan to change plans and the S33 is clearly cheaper | S33 | Same ports, channels and 2.5 Gbps rating |
| You want Xfinity X-Class symmetrical speeds | Neither | Xfinity says customer-owned modems aren't compatible with X-Class |
| You need home phone service from your cable company | Neither | Both are data-only modems with no phone ports |

## How to buy and set up either modem

1. **Check the upload speed on your plan.** If it is far below the download speed, find out from your provider whether faster upload tiers are offered at your address.
2. **Check that your provider supports the exact model.** For Xfinity, check the model in My Device Info, which Xfinity says shows the devices that work with your service, and confirm the device supports your plan's speed. SURFboard says all of its products are "tested and qualified by Cox, Comcast Xfinity, and Charter Spectrum," but your provider's own list is what counts.
3. **Check the price on the day you buy.** We could not confirm a current retail price for either model from an official or retailer page that loaded for us, and third-party marketplace sellers sometimes charge more than the S34's $219 launch MSRP.
4. **Connect a router.** Neither modem has Wi-Fi. Plug your router's WAN port into the modem's 2.5 Gbps port; you need a router with a 2.5 Gbps WAN port to use more than 1 Gbps. For a large home, see our [best Wi-Fi router for a large house guide](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/).
5. **Activate, then return the rental.** Activate the modem with your provider, confirm your speeds, and return any rented modem or gateway so the equipment fee comes off your bill.
