**The ASUS RT-BE86U and RT-BE88U are close relatives: both are dual-band Wi-Fi 7 routers with the same 5 GHz radio, the same processor and no 6 GHz band. The RT-BE88U is the one to pick if you need more wired connections, including a 10G SFP+ port and four extra gigabit LAN ports; otherwise, the RT-BE86U covers the same Wi-Fi ground with fewer ports.** The BE88U also has a 4x4 2.4 GHz radio and twice the RAM, but those matter less for most homes than the port count.

This comparison is based on ASUS's published specifications, checked on November 27, 2026. We have not tested either router.

Source: ASUS tech spec pages for each model. The rates are maximum link (PHY) rates per band, not throughput you'll see on a device. ASUS's class names round up: the BE88U's two bands add to 7,140 Mbps and the BE86U's to 6,796 Mbps.

## Wi-Fi: the 5 GHz radio is the same

For the band most of your phones and laptops will use for fast connections, the two routers match on paper: 4x4, up to 5,764 Mbps, and channels up to 160 MHz. Both support Wi-Fi 7's Multi-Link Operation (MLO) across 2.4 and 5 GHz; ASUS's US page for the BE86U says it supports both STR and EMLSR MLO modes. (Our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO guide](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) explains what those modes do.)

The difference is on 2.4 GHz. The BE88U has four streams on that band to the BE86U's three, for a higher maximum rate (1,376 vs. 1,032 Mbps). ASUS doesn't publish anything showing how much range or speed that adds in a home, so treat it as a minor spec advantage rather than a reason to pay more.

Neither router has a 6 GHz radio, so neither can use Wi-Fi 7's 320 MHz channels or 6 GHz links. If your devices support 6 GHz and you live somewhere crowded, read [Dual-Band Wi-Fi 7: What You Lose Without 6 GHz](/guides/dual-band-wifi-7/) before buying either.

ASUS's US page quotes coverage of up to 2,750 sq. ft. for the RT-BE86U. We didn't find an equivalent figure for the RT-BE88U, so there's no like-for-like coverage comparison to make. For a big home, a mesh system is often the better answer; see our [Wi-Fi router for a large house guide](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/).

## Ports: where the RT-BE88U pulls ahead

This is the real decision.

- **RT-BE86U: five Ethernet ports.** One 10G port (WAN or LAN), one 2.5G port (WAN or LAN) and three 2.5G LAN ports. ASUS says one of the 2.5G LAN ports is set up as a dedicated gaming port. ASUS adds these up to "20G" of total port capacity.
- **RT-BE88U: ten ports.** The same 10G and four 2.5G ports, plus a 10G SFP+ port and four gigabit LAN ports. ASUS adds these up to "34G."

What that means in practice:

- If your home switch or NAS uses **SFP+**, the BE88U can connect to it directly while leaving its 10G RJ45 port free. ASUS's spec sheet doesn't say whether the SFP+ port can serve as the WAN, so check that before relying on it for an SFP+ fiber ONT.
- If you have **many wired devices** (a TV, consoles, a desktop, a NAS, access points), the BE88U's four extra gigabit ports may save you buying a switch.
- **Faster internet plans alone don't favor the BE88U.** Both routers have the same 10G and 2.5G ports, so a multi-gig plan works the same on either; extra ports and SFP+ are what separate them. Our guide to [multi-gig routers and 2.5G and 10G ports](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) covers when faster ports pay off.

The BE86U keeps a second USB port (USB 2.0) that the BE88U drops; both have one USB 3.2 Gen 1 port.

## Processor, memory and software

Both use a 2.6 GHz quad-core processor and 256 MB of flash. The BE88U doubles the RAM to 2 GB. ASUS doesn't publish a performance figure tied to that RAM difference, so we can't say how much it helps; extra memory gives more headroom for features that run on the router, such as VPN, traffic analysis and many connected devices.

The software features ASUS lists are the same for both: AiProtection Pro (Router Security Assessment, Malicious Site Blocking, Two-Way IPS, and Infected Device Prevention and Blocking), AiMesh as either a primary router or a node, and the usual ASUS operating modes, such as access point and media bridge. For the BE86U, ASUS's US page also quotes VPN speeds over Ethernet of up to 300 Mbps with WireGuard and up to 150 Mbps with OpenVPN; we didn't find an equivalent figure for the BE88U.

## Which should you buy?

- **Choose the RT-BE86U** if you'll plug in four or fewer wired devices (one of its five ports goes to your modem or ONT) and you want ASUS's dual-band Wi-Fi 7 hardware without paying for ports you won't use. It's also the lighter unit (854 g vs. 1,060 g).
- **Choose the RT-BE88U** if you need an SFP+ connection, have more than four wired devices and want to avoid adding a switch, or want the extra RAM for running several router features at once.
- **Choose neither** if most of your devices support 6 GHz and your neighborhood's 5 GHz band is congested; a tri-band Wi-Fi 7 router uses that band and these two don't.

We haven't listed prices because holiday-week prices are changing daily and ASUS's US store didn't show a price as visible text when we checked. Compare current prices at the time you buy: the gap between the two is what decides whether the BE88U's extra ports are worth it.
