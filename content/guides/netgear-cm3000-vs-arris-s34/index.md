The NETGEAR Nighthawk CM3000 and the ARRIS SURFboard S34 are the same class of modem: DOCSIS 3.1, one 2.5 Gbps Ethernet port, a rating for speeds up to 2.5 Gbps, and no Wi-Fi or voice. The CM3000 adds support for high-split upload, which NETGEAR's support article said no provider was using as of July 2025, plus a third Ethernet port. **For most people, the S34 is the better buy: it supports mid-split upload and, on Nov. 22, 2026, was about $80 cheaper new ($219.99 at Target vs. $299.99 for a new CM3000 at Staples) and listed in stock at Target, while the new CM3000 was out of stock at NETGEAR.com and for delivery at Staples. Pick the CM3000 if you want high-split headroom for a future plan or need the extra port.**

This comparison is based on NETGEAR's and SURFboard's product pages, data sheets and support articles, plus retailer listings checked on Nov. 22, 2026. We have not tested either modem.

## What is the real difference: mid-split vs. high-split?

The difference is how much of the cable line each modem can use for upload. A cable line divides its frequencies between download and upload. The S34 data sheet lists a "Switchable Diplexer US 5-42MHz/5-85MHz," meaning the S34 can upload on the traditional 5 to 42 MHz range (low-split) or on the wider 5 to 85 MHz range (mid-split). More upload spectrum lets a provider sell faster upload tiers.

The CM3000 goes one step further. NETGEAR says it "is prepared for today and tomorrow with both mid/split and high/split technology." NETGEAR's data sheet doesn't give the CM3000's frequency ranges.

The catch: NETGEAR's support article on mid/high-split, last updated July 7, 2025, said no ISP was using high-split yet. It says high-split "is not currently used by any ISPs but is expected to be used in the future." The CM3000 data sheet says high-split "may be offered by ISPs in the near future," and that mid-split "is currently being offered by some ISPs in some regions." NETGEAR also notes: "Your modem and ISP must both support a frequency split for you to be able to use it."

So where a provider offers mid-split upload, both modems can use it. The CM3000's extra is insurance for a high-split plan that, by NETGEAR's account, providers hadn't launched. ## Do the extra Ethernet ports matter?

Usually not. In a common setup, one modem port connects to your Wi-Fi router's WAN port and everything else connects to the router. On a plan faster than 1 Gbps, the 2.5 Gbps port is the simplest way to pass the full speed, provided your router also has a 2.5 Gbps or faster WAN port.

The CM3000's two Gigabit ports add one option the S34 lacks: NETGEAR says you can "connect the two Gigabit ports to a router with link aggregation for up to 2Gbps." (Another line on the same page says up to 2.5 Gbps for this setup, so we use the lower figure.) That only helps if your router supports link aggregation on its WAN side.

## Will they work with my cable provider?

Probably, but check the exact model first. NETGEAR says the CM3000 "works seamlessly with every major U.S. cable service provider," and its product page lists Xfinity's 2 Gbps download / 200 Mbps upload tier among the plans it can access. SURFboard says the S34 is "Compatible with Cox, Spectrum, Xfinity and other major US cable internet providers," and adds: "Compatibility may vary by model and provider."

Your provider's list is the one that counts. Xfinity says "Your modem must work with Xfinity and support your internet speed," and points customers to My Device Info, where you sign in or enter your address. NETGEAR's own support article on which of its modems are certified by cable providers (last updated July 7, 2025) doesn't list the CM3000, so don't treat it as the final word either way.

## Before you buy either modem

1. **Check your plan's upload speed.** If your provider offers mid-split upload tiers at your address, both modems support mid-split. Ask whether any high-split plan is offered before paying extra for it.
2. **Check the model with your provider.** For Xfinity, use My Device Info.
3. **Check that you don't need voice.** Neither modem supports cable phone service.
4. **Check the price on the day you buy.** Prices above were checked on Nov. 22, 2026; buy new from an authorized reseller, since NETGEAR says its limited warranty "is valid only if purchased from a NETGEAR authorized reseller."
5. **Pair it with the right router.** To use more than 1 Gbps, your router needs a 2.5 Gbps WAN port (or, with the CM3000, link aggregation support).
