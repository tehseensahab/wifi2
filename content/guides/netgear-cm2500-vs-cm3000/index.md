The NETGEAR Nighthawk CM2500 and CM3000 are nearly the same modem. Both are DOCSIS 3.1 with the same channel counts, both support mid-split and high-split upload, and neither supports cable voice. **The difference is the ports. The CM3000 has a 2.5 Gbps Ethernet port; the CM2500 has two 1 Gbps ports and can pass at most 1 Gbps to a router unless that router supports internet link aggregation. If your plan is 900 Mbps or slower, the CM2500 is enough and was listed $50 cheaper at NETGEAR.com on Nov. 23, 2026 (both were out of stock there). If your plan is 1 Gbps or faster, or might be soon, get the CM3000, unless your router supports internet link aggregation.**

This comparison is based on NETGEAR's data sheets, product pages and support article, plus a retailer listing checked on Nov. 23, 2026. We have not tested either modem.

## The real difference: one 2.5 Gbps port vs. two 1 Gbps ports

Each 1 Gbps Ethernet port carries at most 1 Gbps, and a bit less in practice. That is the main difference between these two modems.

**CM3000:** plug a router with a 2.5 Gbps (or faster) WAN port into the modem's 2.5 Gbps port. NETGEAR says to connect "your router's multi-gig Internet port directly into the modem's 2.5Gig port." That is the simple path to more than 1 Gbps. If you're unsure what your router has, see [do you need a multi-gig router?](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/)

**CM2500:** the only way past 1 Gbps is link aggregation. NETGEAR's data sheet says to "get multi-gig speeds up to 2Gbps‡ by connecting the two 1 Gig ports to a router that supports internet with link aggregation." That means your router has to combine two of its ports into one internet connection. If your router has a 2.5 Gbps WAN port but no internet link aggregation, the CM2500 can feed it at only 1 Gbps. Check your router's spec sheet for internet (WAN) link aggregation before counting on 2 Gbps.

NETGEAR's own product pages reflect this. The CM2500 page lists Xfinity at "900Mbps, 100Mbps," Spectrum at "900Mbps, 35Mbps" and Cox at "900Mbps, 100Mbps." The CM3000 page lists Xfinity at "2Gbps, 200Mbps," Spectrum at "1Gbps, 35Mbps" and Cox at "1Gbps, 100Mbps."

The CM3000 also keeps its two Gigabit ports with link aggregation, so it supports either connection style.

## Does mid-split or high-split change the choice?

No. Both modems support both. NETGEAR's data sheets use the same sentence for each: the modem "is prepared for today and tomorrow with both mid-split and high-split technology."

What matters is whether your provider uses them. NETGEAR's CM2500 data sheet says mid-split "is currently being offered by some ISPs in some regions." Its support article, last updated July 7, 2025, says high-split "is not currently used by any ISPs but is expected to be used in the future," and that "your modem and ISP must both support a frequency split for you to be able to use it." For more on what the splits mean for upload speed, see our [CM3000 vs. ARRIS S34 comparison](/guides/netgear-cm3000-vs-arris-s34/).

## Before you buy

1. **Check your plan speed.** If it's 1 Gbps or faster, the CM2500 needs link aggregation to keep up. Not sure what your current modem supports? See [what DOCSIS version is my modem](/guides/what-docsis-is-my-modem/).
2. **Check your provider accepts the exact model.** NETGEAR's CM2500 data sheet says to "check with your cable provider to confirm this NETGEAR modem is allowed on your cable network." For Xfinity, our [best modem for Xfinity](/guides/best-modem-for-xfinity/) guide covers how to check.
3. **Check you don't need voice.** Neither model supports cable phone service.
4. **Buy from an authorized reseller.** NETGEAR's product pages say its limited warranty is valid only if purchased from a NETGEAR authorized reseller and you register the device. Refurbished units from NETGEAR carry a 90-day warranty.
5. **Check the price on the day.** Both new models were out of stock at NETGEAR.com when we checked on Nov. 23, 2026.
