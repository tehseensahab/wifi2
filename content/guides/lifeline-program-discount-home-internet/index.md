Lifeline is a federal program that takes up to $9.25 a month off a phone, internet or bundled service bill for eligible households in the United States. You can qualify through income or through enrollment in certain assistance programs, you apply through a national portal, and then you pick a participating provider. Each household gets one Lifeline benefit, and you must confirm your eligibility every year.

This is an explainer based on the program administrator's pages and FCC documents. We have not tested any Lifeline provider's plans, and available plans differ by address.

## How much does Lifeline save?

The Universal Service Administrative Company (USAC), which runs the program for the FCC, states the amounts on its Lifeline pages:

| Who | Monthly Lifeline discount | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Most eligible subscribers | Up to $9.25 | Phone, internet or bundled service |
| Eligible subscribers on qualifying Tribal lands | Up to $34.25 | Phone, internet or bundled service |

The key words are "up to." Lifeline is a discount from a participating provider's price, not a payment to you. A simple example, using made-up numbers: if a provider's home internet plan costs $50 a month before taxes and fees, and you receive the full $9.25, the plan would cost $40.75 before taxes and fees. Providers' prices and plans vary by address, so check the price after the discount, not before it.

## Who qualifies?

According to the Lifeline Support site, you can qualify in either of two ways.

**By income.** Your household income must be at or below 135% of the 2026 Federal Poverty Guidelines. The site lists these figures for a one-person household: $21,546 a year in the 48 contiguous states, Washington, DC, and the territories; $26,933 in Alaska; and $24,786 in Hawaii. Each additional person adds $7,668, $9,585 or $8,816 respectively.

Applying that addition to the contiguous-states figure gives these annual income ceilings, which are our calculation from the published figures and not a table copied from the program:

| Household size | Annual income limit (48 contiguous states and DC) |
|---|---|
| 1 person | $21,546 |
| 2 people | $29,214 |
| 3 people | $36,882 |
| 4 people | $44,550 |

**By participating in a qualifying program.** The Lifeline Support site lists Medicaid; the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP); Supplemental Security Income (SSI); Federal Public Housing Assistance (including Section 8 vouchers); and the Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit. It says participation by a child or dependent in one of these programs can also qualify the household. Additional programs apply on Tribal lands, and separate rules apply to survivors of domestic violence and certain other crimes; the site's qualification page has the details.

## How do you apply?

1. **Apply through the National Verifier** at lifelinesupport.org. You will need documents showing your income or program participation. Residents of Oregon and Texas apply through their state's website instead.
2. **Contact a participating phone or internet company** after you are approved, and ask it to apply the benefit. Lifeline Support says that after approval, you contact a participating company to receive the benefit.
3. **Recertify every year.** USAC, or your state in Oregon and Texas, checks that you still qualify. If you miss a recertification deadline, you lose the benefit, and your service may be ended or your bill may rise.

## What are the limits?

**One benefit per household.** Under the FCC's rule at 47 CFR 54.409(c), there must not be anyone else in the subscriber's household subscribed to a Lifeline service. If you use Lifeline on a phone line, you cannot also use it on home internet. Decide which one saves you more.

**Data allowances are set by the FCC.** In a notice released July 29, 2026 (DA 26-796), the FCC's Wireline Competition Bureau set these Lifeline minimum service standards, which it said will continue until December 1, 2027:

| Service | Minimum standard |
|---|---|
| Fixed broadband (home internet) | 1,280 GB of data a month |
| Mobile broadband | 4.5 GB of data a month |
| Mobile voice | 1,000 minutes a month |

The notice explains that a one-year waiver issued July 1, 2026 paused scheduled increases to the fixed and mobile data standards. The notice we read does not state a minimum speed for fixed broadband, so we cannot tell you what speed a Lifeline home internet plan must deliver. Ask the provider for the plan's download and upload speeds, and compare it with what your household needs using our guide on [how much internet speed you need]({{< relref "/guides/how-much-internet-speed-do-i-need" >}}).

**Availability depends on your address.** Lifeline is available only from participating providers, and which ones serve you varies. If fiber, cable or 5G home internet are all options at your address, see our comparison of [fiber, cable and 5G home internet]({{< relref "/guides/fiber-vs-cable-vs-5g-home-internet" >}}) before choosing.

## Check your equipment costs too

A Lifeline discount applies to service. It does not tell you what a provider charges to rent a router or gateway. If you are weighing rental fees against your own hardware, our guide on [buying your own router vs. using your provider's]({{< relref "/guides/buy-your-own-router-vs-isp-equipment" >}}) explains how to compare the monthly cost.

## Bottom line

If your household income is at or below the 2026 limit or you take part in a qualifying program, apply through the National Verifier, then ask a participating provider for its post-discount price. Remember that you get one benefit per household, and put your yearly recertification date in your calendar.
