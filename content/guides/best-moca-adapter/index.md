The best MoCA adapter for most homes is a **MoCA 2.5 adapter with a 2.5 Gbps Ethernet port**, and two models fit that description: the **goCoax MA2500D**, listed at $60 per adapter on goCoax's own store on November 7, 2026, and the **ASUS MA-25**, though we couldn't confirm a US price for it that day. The **Motorola MM1025** has the same headline specs plus a point-of-entry filter and a two-year warranty, but it was out of stock at Walmart and HSN when we checked. If your router, switch and devices only have gigabit Ethernet ports, the **TRENDnet TMO-312C** is enough, and TRENDnet backs it with a three-year warranty.

This is a research-based guide. We haven't tested these adapters. Specs come from the makers' data sheets and spec pages; prices and stock were checked on retailer and maker websites on November 7, 2026. "Up to 2.5 Gbps" is a maker's maximum, not a speed you should expect through every splitter in your house.

All five are MoCA 2.5 adapters. The MoCA Alliance's April 2016 announcement says MoCA 2.5 is "capable of up to 2.5 Gbps actual data rates" and is "backward interoperable with MoCA 2.0 and MoCA 1.1," so any of them can join a network that already has older MoCA 2.0 or 1.1 gear. Expect links to older adapters to run at the older standard's speeds.

## How to choose a MoCA adapter

Three things decide which adapter fits, and the "up to 2.5 Gbps" headline speed is not one of them.

### 1. Match the Ethernet port to what you'll plug in

The adapter's Ethernet port is a hard ceiling. A gigabit port can't pass more than 1 Gbps to the device on the other end, however fast the coax link is. That matters in two cases:

- **Your internet plan is faster than 1 Gbps**, and you want the full speed on a wired PC or a mesh node in another room.
- **You're using MoCA as mesh backhaul** and the mesh nodes have 2.5 Gbps ports. Our guide to [wired vs. wireless mesh backhaul](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/) explains why a wired link helps.

If your plan is 1 Gbps or slower and nothing in the house has a port faster than gigabit, a 2.5 Gbps adapter adds little today. If you're not sure what your router has, our [multi-gig router guide](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) explains how to check.

### 2. Count the adapters

You need one adapter next to the router and one in every room you want to wire. Most people start with two. Some ISP gateways and routers have MoCA built in, and the Motorola data sheet notes that "up to 16 MoCA-enabled devices can be connected to a MoCA-enabled router or modem/router." If yours does, it can stand in for the adapter at the router end. Check its spec sheet before you buy a 2-pack.

### 3. Budget for a point-of-entry filter

A point-of-entry (PoE) filter goes where the coax enters your home and keeps the MoCA signal inside your network. Motorola's MM1025 data sheet is the only one of these five that lists a "PoE Filter" in the box. goCoax, ASUS, TRENDnet and the ScreenBeam user guide don't list one, so plan on buying one separately for those. (This "PoE" is not Power over Ethernet.)

One more check: if satellite TV runs over your coax, check compatibility first. goCoax's MA2500D product page warns that MoCA can overlap in frequency with satellite TV and asks buyers to check before ordering.

## goCoax MA2500D: best for most homes

The MA2500D is a single MoCA 2.5 adapter with one 2.5 Gbps Ethernet port.

**What goCoax lists:** "MoCA2.5 over Coax," "1 x 2.5GbE RJ45 LAN" and "Up to 2.5Gbps actual data throughput." The package is one adapter ("1x MA2500D").

**Price and stock:** goCoax showed $60.00, down from a $74.99 regular price, for one adapter on November 7, 2026. Two adapters at that price come to $120.

**Who it's for:** anyone with a 2.5 Gbps port on the router or mesh node who wants a single, inexpensive building block they can add to one room at a time.

**What to know:** goCoax's product page doesn't state a frequency range, a warranty or whether a PoE filter is included, so check before ordering if those matter to you.

## ASUS MA-25: the big-brand 2.5 Gbps alternative

**What ASUS lists on its US spec page:** "MoCA 2.5/2.0," "2.5Gbps MoCA to Ethernet (Full duplex)" and an "RJ-45 port for 2.5Gbase-T Ethernet." Security is listed as "MoCA protected setup (MPS)."

**What's different:** ASUS lists two operating modes: "Mode 1(4CH) : 1,275 ~ 1,675MHz" and "Mode 2(5CH) : 1,125 ~ 1,625MHz." Motorola, TRENDnet and ScreenBeam list a single 1,125 to 1,675 MHz range; goCoax doesn't state one. If you mix an MA-25 with another brand, check which mode it's using; ASUS's documentation covers the setting.

**Who it's for:** people who prefer buying from a major networking brand, or who already use ASUS routers.

**What to know:** ASUS's spec page doesn't list package contents, so we couldn't confirm whether a PoE filter is included. We didn't find a US retailer page with a confirmed price on November 7, 2026, so we've left the price out.

## Motorola MM1025: filter included, if you can find it

**What Motorola lists:** "MoCA 2.5 for Ethernet over Coax," "One (1) RJ45 2.5Gbps Ethernet port," a "MoCA Ext Band D operation: 1125 - 1675 MHz" frequency range and a "two year warranty." The package contents include a "PoE Filter." Motorola sells it as the MM1025 (1-pack) and MM2025 (2-pack).

**The honest speed note:** Motorola's data sheet says speeds are "up to 2.5 Gbps," with a footnote: "Actual speeds may be lower than 2.5 Gbps due to signal losses from Coax splitters and cables." That applies to every adapter here, but Motorola's is the only data sheet we reviewed that spells it out in a footnote.

**Price and stock:** Walmart listed the MM1025 at $79.00 but "Out of stock" on November 7, 2026. HSN listed it at $119.99 and "sold out." Until it's back in stock at a normal price, the goCoax or ASUS adapter plus a separately bought filter is the more practical buy.

## TRENDnet TMO-312C: enough for gigabit networks

**What TRENDnet lists:** "Supports MoCA®2.5 over coaxial cables," "1 x RJ-45 Gigabit LAN port," "Bonded MoCA 2.5 channels provide a net throughput of up to 2.5Gbps," support for "up to 16 nodes on one network," a 1,125 to 1,675 MHz range, and a "3-year" warranty.

**The catch:** the coax side is MoCA 2.5, but the Ethernet port is gigabit. Each device you plug into a TMO-312C gets 1 Gbps at most. That's fine for a gigabit plan, a game console or a TV, and it isn't a bottleneck if your router has no faster port anyway.

**What to know:** the box contents TRENDnet lists are the adapter, a power adapter and a quick installation guide, with no PoE filter. We didn't confirm a current price.

## ScreenBeam ECB7250: in stock as a 2-pack, with a spec question

ScreenBeam's ECB7250 is sold under the Actiontec name at B&H, which showed the 2-pack (ECB7250K02) "In Stock" on November 7, 2026, though the page didn't display a price.

**The spec question:** B&H says the adapter "connects to your router or other wired device's 2.5G compatible RJ45 port." ScreenBeam's own user guide contradicts itself: its features list says "Ethernet port (2.5 gigabit)," while its specifications table says "LAN Ethernet Port 10/100/1000 (1)," which is gigabit. We couldn't resolve that from a primary source, so if you need 2.5 Gbps, confirm the port speed with the seller before buying.

**Other details from the user guide:** a 1,125 to 1,675 MHz range, a "one-year Limited Hardware Warranty," and a note that "by default, MoCA encryption is disabled." The guide lists no PoE filter in the box.

## Is MoCA the right fix?

MoCA makes sense when you already have coax outlets in the rooms that need a connection and can't run Ethernet. If the coax is missing or in the wrong rooms, [powerline adapters](/guides/are-powerline-adapters-worth-it/) or a mesh system are the alternatives. If you're deciding between a wired adapter and Wi-Fi hardware, our [access point vs. extender vs. mesh guide](/guides/access-point-vs-extender-vs-mesh/) covers what each approach can do with a wired link like MoCA.

## Bottom line

Buy two MoCA 2.5 adapters with 2.5 Gbps ports if your router or mesh has a 2.5 Gbps port; the goCoax MA2500D was the easiest to buy at a clear price on November 7, 2026, and the ASUS MA-25 is the big-brand option. Add a point-of-entry filter unless your kit includes one, check compatibility if satellite TV shares the coax, and treat "up to 2.5 Gbps" as a ceiling, not a promise. If everything you own tops out at gigabit, the TRENDnet TMO-312C does the job.
