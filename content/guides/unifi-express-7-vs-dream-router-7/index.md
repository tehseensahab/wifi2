**Buy the Dream Router 7 if you need wired ports, PoE or UniFi Protect cameras; buy the UniFi Express 7 if you mostly need Wi-Fi, want a 10 GbE copper WAN port, or plan to mesh several units.** Ubiquiti lists both as 6-stream Wi-Fi 7 gateways with 6 GHz support and the same 2.3 Gbps IDS/IPS throughput, so Wi-Fi speed isn't the deciding factor. On November 14, 2026, Ubiquiti's US store listed the Dream Router 7 (UDR7) at $279 and the Express 7 (UX7) as a 2-pack at $379.

This comparison is based on Ubiquiti's store pages and announcements, a retailer listing and an independent review. We haven't tested either device ourselves.

## The short version

| If you... | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Have a desktop PC, NAS, TV or console to wire in | Dream Router 7 | Three 2.5 GbE LAN ports (plus a 2.5 GbE WAN) vs. one LAN port on the Express 7 |
| Want to add UniFi cameras | Dream Router 7 | microSD storage for Protect; Express 7 runs only the Network app (per Dong Knows Tech) |
| Need to power an access point or camera from the router | Dream Router 7 | One PoE output port (15.4 W per Adorama's listing) |
| Have a modem or ONT with a 10 GbE copper port | Express 7 | 10 GbE RJ45 WAN; the Dream Router 7's 10G port is SFP+ |
| Want to cover a larger home with matching mesh nodes | Express 7 | Sold in multi-packs; works as a USB-C-powered mesh node |
| Want one small box for an apartment | Either | Same Wi-Fi radios; pick by ports |

## Wi-Fi: effectively the same

Ubiquiti's [February 2025 launch post](https://blog.ui.com/article/introducing-all-new-10-gigabit-unifi-cloud-gateways) describes the Dream Router 7 as an "Integrated 6-stream WiFi 7 AP with 6 GHz support" and the Express 7 as "Integrated 6-stream WiFi 7 with 6 GHz support." Adorama's [Dream Router 7 listing](https://www.adorama.com/ubudr7us.html) breaks that down as 2x2 on each band, at up to 688 Mbps on 2.4 GHz, 4.3 Gbps on 5 GHz and 5.7 Gbps on 6 GHz. [Dong Knows Tech's Express 7 review](https://dongknows.com/ubiquiti-ux7-unifi-express-7-review) lists the same three figures for the Express 7 and says the Dream Router 7 "has the same Wi-Fi specs."

Those are maximum link rates, not the speed you'll see on a laptop or phone. Real throughput depends on the client, distance and walls.

Ubiquiti's comparison table lists the same coverage area for both, 160 m² (1,750 sq ft), and Adorama lists that figure for the Dream Router 7. (Ubiquiti's table doesn't label its columns, so we matched them to models by card order.) For a bigger home, that points you toward more than one unit, which is where the Express 7 has an edge.

## Ports: the real difference

**Dream Router 7.** Ubiquiti lists a 10G SFP+ port and a 2.5 GbE RJ45 port for WAN, plus three 2.5 GbE LAN ports, one of them PoE. Ubiquiti's comparison table puts the maximum WAN count at four (its columns are unlabeled, so we matched them by card order). Adorama says the PoE port supplies 15.4 W. Check that against the power draw of any access point or camera you plan to run from it.

**Express 7.** Ubiquiti lists one 10 GbE RJ45 WAN port and "(1) 2.5 GbE RJ45 LAN port to extend network." Ubiquiti's launch table lists no PoE output, and as a mesh node it can run from USB-C power.

What that means at home:

- **More than one wired device?** With the Express 7 you'll need a switch on its single LAN port. The Dream Router 7 has three LAN ports built in.
- **Multi-gig internet?** The Express 7's 10 GbE WAN is a standard copper (RJ45) port. The Dream Router 7's 10G WAN is SFP+, which needs an SFP+ module or direct-attach cable; otherwise you use its 2.5 GbE WAN port. Our [multi-gig router guide](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) explains when ports above 2.5 Gbps matter.
- **Backup internet?** With up to four WANs on the Dream Router 7 and one on the Express 7 (per Ubiquiti's comparison table), the Dream Router 7 is the better fit for two wired ISPs. Our [dual WAN guide](/guides/best-dual-wan-router/) covers failover options.

## Apps: Network only, or the full UniFi suite

Ubiquiti says "The UDR7 can seamlessly run all UniFi applications at once" and has "SD-Card storage for Protect and other UniFi applications." Adorama lists a "Pre-installed 64GB microSD for NVR," so you can record UniFi Protect cameras without buying a separate recorder.

For the Express 7, Ubiquiti's launch post says it runs "UniFi Network, like the first Express," and doesn't mention Protect. Dong Knows Tech says it's "restricted to the Network application within the UniFi Application Suite." If cameras are in your plans, that settles it.

## Mesh: where the Express 7 fits better

Ubiquiti says the Express 7 can "Serve as a Cloud Gateway," "Be adopted as a wired AP," or "Extend coverage as a mesh AP, just power via USB-C." In September 2026, Ubiquiti launched [Whole-Home WiFi by UniFi](https://blog.ui.com/article/introducing-whole-home-wifi-by-unifi), which lets you "Add more Express 7 nodes instantly for additional coverage with no reconfiguration," and says there are "No mandatory apps."

Dong Knows Tech's review adds that on September 2, 2026, with UniFi OS 5.2 and the latest Network app, the Express 7 "gained the STR MLO feature when operating as a mesh node." MLO (multi-link operation) lets a Wi-Fi 7 link use more than one band at once; our [MLO explainer](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) covers what it does. We haven't confirmed that change in Ubiquiti's own release notes, so treat it as the reviewer's report.

If you need whole-home coverage, our [large-house router guide](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/) compares single routers with mesh systems.

The Express 7 2-pack costs $100 more than one Dream Router 7 ($379 vs. $279), which works out to $189.50 per Express 7 unit. With the listed surcharges, the gap is $110 ($417 vs. $307). If one router won't cover your home, the 2-pack gives you two units for that extra $100, but you give up the Dream Router 7's extra ports, PoE and Protect support.

## Which should you buy?

- **Buy the Dream Router 7** for a home office, a house with several wired devices, or any setup that might add UniFi cameras. It's the more complete single box.
- **Buy the Express 7** if your home is mostly Wi-Fi, your modem or ONT has a 10 GbE copper port, or you want matching mesh nodes from the start.
