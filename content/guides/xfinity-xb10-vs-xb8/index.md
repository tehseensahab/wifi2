**The Xfinity XB10 is the gateway to want if your plan is faster than 1 Gbps, you have wired devices with multi-gig ports, or you own Wi-Fi 7 phones and laptops; on plans of 1 Gbps or slower, the Wi-Fi 6E XB8's lower ceiling matters less.** Xfinity rates the XB10 at 10 Gbps maximum throughput with two 10 Gbps Ethernet ports, and the XB8 at 2.5 Gbps. A third model, the XB9, is a Wi-Fi 7 gateway on DOCSIS 3.1 with no phone port, which matters if you have Xfinity Voice.

This is a research-based guide built from Xfinity and Comcast pages checked on November 12, 2026. We haven't tested these gateways, and the speed figures below are Xfinity's ratings, not measurements.

## XB10 vs. XB9 vs. XB8 at a glance

| | XB10 | XB9 | XB8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model numbers | SG417DBCT, CGM601TCOM, CGM601TCM | CWA438TCOM | CGM4981COM |
| Wi-Fi | Wi-Fi 7, tri-band | Wi-Fi 7, tri-band | Wi-Fi 6E, tri-band |
| Cable standard | DOCSIS 4.0 | DOCSIS 3.1 | Not listed on Xfinity's page |
| Xfinity's speed figure | 10 Gbps maximum throughput | Network bandwidth up to 2 Gbps down, 300 Mbps up | 2.5 Gbps maximum throughput |
| Ethernet ports | 4: two 10 Gbps, two 1 Gbps | 3: one 5 Gbps, two 1 Gbps | 4 (no 10 Gbps ports listed) |
| Phone ports | 1 | 0 (no Xfinity Voice) | 2 (Xfinity notes a separate alarm port) |
| Voice battery backup capability | No | No phone port | Yes |

Source: Xfinity's gateway overview page, last modified October 29, 2026. "Maximum data throughput" is a device rating, not your plan speed; in our view, Wi-Fi speeds to any single device will typically be lower. The XB9's figure is listed as network bandwidth, not a throughput rating.

## How to tell which gateway you have

Xfinity says to "review the equipment’s bottom panel, visit your Devices page on xfinity.com or use the Xfinity app." The model number on the label maps to the names above: CGM4981COM is an XB8, CWA438TCOM is an XB9, and SG417DBCT, CGM601TCOM or CGM601TCM is an XB10.

## What the XB10 offers

**DOCSIS 4.0.** Xfinity says the XB10 "is the first Xfinity device to combine Unified DOCSIS 4.0 technology with the WiFi 7 (802.11be) standard." Comcast announced DOCSIS 4.0 in 2023 as its route to "multi-gigabit symmetrical speeds to customers over the connections that already exist." Xfinity's page doesn't list a DOCSIS version for the XB8. Whether those plans are offered at your address is a separate question. Our [DOCSIS 3.0 vs. 3.1 vs. 4.0 guide](/guides/docsis-3-0-vs-3-1-vs-4-0-cable-modem/) explains the standards.

**Wi-Fi 7 and 6 GHz.** Comcast says the XB10 gives "access to the 6 GHz band" and can power "up to 300 connected devices in the home." The XB8 has 6 GHz too, as a Wi-Fi 6E gateway. Wi-Fi 7's extras, such as Multi-Link Operation, only help devices that support Wi-Fi 7, and Xfinity's spec list doesn't say which Wi-Fi 7 features the XB10 supports. Our [Wi-Fi 6E vs. Wi-Fi 7 guide](/guides/wifi-6e-vs-wifi-7/) covers the difference.

**10 Gbps Ethernet.** This is the clearest hardware gap. Of these gateways, only the XB10 has 10 Gbps ports (two of them); the XB9 has one 5 Gbps port, and Xfinity lists no multi-gig ports for the XB8, which it rates at 2.5 Gbps overall. If you plug in a multi-gig PC, NAS or your own mesh system, that's the difference that shows.

## Where the XB9 fits

Xfinity calls the XB9 "a DOCSIS 3.1 WiFi 7 gateway designed for eligible households." It gives you Wi-Fi 7 and a 5 Gbps LAN port, but Xfinity lists its network bandwidth as "up to 2 Gbps downstream and 300 Mbps upstream," a lower ceiling than the XB10.

The bigger catch is phone service. Xfinity says "the XB9 Gateway cannot be used with Xfinity Voice because it does not have a phone port," and that "members with Xfinity Voice should receive an XB8 or XB10 Gateway." Xfinity adds that the XB9 "is also not compatible with Xfinity TV service when all TV Boxes in the home are XiD or Xi3 models."

## If you have Xfinity Voice: the battery backup difference

Xfinity's spec list shows "Battery Backup capability (Xfinity Voice Only)" as "Yes" for the XB8 and "No" for the XB10. If you rely on a home phone line during power cuts, that's a reason to ask Xfinity before swapping an XB8 for an XB10.

## Should you upgrade from an XB8 to an XB10?

**Upgrade makes sense if:**

- Your plan is faster than 1 Gbps, or you're moving to one of Comcast's DOCSIS 4.0 multi-gig plans (our reading: the XB8 has no listed multi-gig ports and a 2.5 Gbps rating).
- You have wired devices or a mesh system with 2.5 GbE, 5 GbE or 10 GbE ports.
- Several of your phones, laptops or consoles support Wi-Fi 7.

**The XB8 is probably fine if:**

- Your plan is 1 Gbps or slower and most devices are on Wi-Fi 6 or 6E.
- You use Xfinity Voice and want the battery backup option Xfinity lists for the XB8.

For a free swap, don't count on xFi Complete's Tech Upgrade program. It covers customers who subscribed to xFi Complete "prior to December 31, 2024," provides "a newer gateway three years after subscribing," and says "the upgraded Gateway will not necessarily be an upgrade to our then most advanced Gateway." Its terms also say that if your current gateway is "Comcast's XB8 model or newer," then "the Gateway will not be upgraded." Instead, Xfinity says: "If you rent one of Xfinity's devices and wonder whether it's time to update to a newer model, visit xfinity.com/equipmentupdate."

If you'd rather stop renting, our [best modem for Xfinity guide](/guides/best-modem-for-xfinity/) covers buying your own. Xfinity says renting includes access to its hotspots and that "xFi is not compatible with third-party equipment."

## Do all three work with Xfinity Pro?

Yes. Xfinity says "the Xfinity Pro WiFi Extender works with Xfinity Gateways XB8, XB9, and XB10 devices," and that Pro subscribers "will receive a compatible gateway based on their network type and internet speed."
