Start with Rely if your household mostly streams, browses and makes video calls: it is the cheapest of T-Mobile's three main unlimited 5G plans and has the same listed upload speed and latency as the pricier plans. Pick Amplified, $10 a month more, if you regularly need more download speed; T-Mobile lists its typical download range at 170 to 498 Mbps against Rely's 133 to 415 Mbps. Pick All-In, another $10, only if you want the extras: a mesh extender when the gateway alone won't cover your home, live tech support, security software and a 10-year price guarantee.

This comparison uses T-Mobile's own plan pages, Broadband Facts labels and speed disclosures, checked on November 19, 2026. It is research-based: we have not measured these plans ourselves, and the speeds are T-Mobile's projections, not our tests. Prices change often, so confirm them at checkout. T-Mobile also sells other plans (such as Lite and Backup) that are outside this comparison. If you are still deciding between 5G and a wired connection, read our [5G home internet vs. cable guide](/guides/5g-home-internet-vs-cable/) first.

## How much do T-Mobile Home Internet plans cost?

On November 19, 2026, T-Mobile's Broadband Facts labels listed single-line monthly prices of $60 for Rely, $70 for Amplified and $80 for All-In. Those labels say the price "does not include AutoPay or other discounts." With AutoPay the plans page showed $50, $60 and $70. The AutoPay discount requires a bank account or debit card; "otherwise $10 more/line/mo."

Adding a T-Mobile phone line lowers the price further, but the bundle offers on T-Mobile's pages were not consistent with one another when we checked. The All-In plan page, for example, said "Get All-In for just $55/month with AutoPay & any T-Mobile phone plan," calling it "a $15/month savings when you bundle." Check the price for your own account at checkout.

Other charges on every plan, per the Broadband Facts labels: a $35 device connection charge, a $0 early termination fee, government taxes that vary by location, and a 0.749% recovery fee in Texas only.

## How fast is each T-Mobile Home Internet plan?

T-Mobile's speed disclosure lists these typical ranges for 5G service. It says they are "projections based on roughly the 25th and 75th percentiles of network tests," so roughly a quarter of tests come in below the low number. On the same day, T-Mobile's Rely plan card showed a narrower 133 to 354 Mbps; we use the speed disclosure page, which the All-In plan page also matches.

- **Amplified and All-In:** download 170 to 498 Mbps, upload 12 to 55 Mbps, latency 16 to 28 ms.
- **Rely:** download 133 to 415 Mbps, upload 12 to 55 Mbps, latency 16 to 28 ms.

The difference is download only. Amplified's range starts 37 Mbps higher and tops out 83 Mbps higher than Rely's. Upload speed and latency are identical, so on paper Amplified won't improve upload-heavy tasks such as cloud backup, or latency. T-Mobile also describes Amplified and All-In as having its "fastest available speeds with no speed caps," wording it does not use for Rely.

What you actually get depends on the 5G signal at your address. The same disclosure says the ranges reflect "network performance nationally on recent devices."

## Do the plans get different priority during congestion?

T-Mobile doesn't say they do. Its plan terms attach the same note to Rely, Amplified and All-In: "During congestion, customers on this plan may notice speeds lower than other customers," with "further reduction if using >1.2TB/mo., due to data prioritization." Paying for a higher plan raises the listed speed range, but T-Mobile does not say higher tiers get higher priority on a busy cell site. If steady evening speeds matter most, compare against cable or fiber in our [5G vs. cable guide](/guides/5g-home-internet-vs-cable/).

## What does All-In add for the extra $10?

T-Mobile says All-In "includes all the same benefits of Amplified," and lists the same typical speeds. The extra $10 a month over Amplified buys:

- **Mesh Wi-Fi extender:** "up to 1 mesh extenders as needed based on professional installer assessment," requested after activation. This can matter most in larger or multi-floor homes where one gateway leaves dead zones.
- **Live tech support:** help with "setup, configuration, troubleshooting, and everyday use," provided by an Assurant company under third-party terms.
- **Advanced Cyber Security:** T-Mobile says it "proactively monitors and helps block threats on your home network and connected devices," managed in the T-Life app.
- **Gateway upgrade:** "one-time upgrade to the latest gateway generation after year three, subject to availability," for qualifying new All-In lines after 11/14/25.
- **10-year price guarantee** instead of 5 years.

Streaming is no longer part of the deal: T-Mobile's All-In page says "The new All-In doesn't include Hulu or Paramount+ on us."

If you already own a good mesh system or router, the mesh extender is less valuable. ## What does the price guarantee cover?

T-Mobile says it "won't change the price of fixed-wireless 5G internet data" for at least the guarantee period: 10 years on All-In, 5 years on Rely and Amplified. The guarantee starts when you activate or switch to an eligible plan and does not restart if you add a line or change plans. It excludes taxes and fees, voluntary equipment or speed upgrades, future wireless generations, select limited-time promotions, per-use charges, third-party services and network management practices. In other words, the plan price is protected, but fees, promotions and congestion management are not.

## Which T-Mobile Home Internet plan should you choose?

| If you... | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly stream, browse and video call | Rely | Same upload and latency as the others for the lowest price |
| Have several people streaming or downloading at once | Amplified | Higher typical download range for $10 a month ($120 a year) more |
| Have a large or multi-floor home with weak spots | All-In | Includes a mesh extender as needed |
| Want live help and the longest price protection | All-In | Tech support, security software and a 10-year guarantee |
| Already own a mesh system you like | Rely or Amplified | All-In's main extra is hardware you already have |
| Host game servers or need port forwarding | Check first | T-Mobile's Home Internet FAQ does not say whether you get a public IP; see our [CGNAT explainer](/guides/what-is-cgnat/) |

Whichever plan you pick, T-Mobile's 15-day Test Drive gives you "a one-time bill credit" to zero out service charges if you cancel within 15 days. You must return the equipment within 45 days of cancelling or face a non-return fee of up to $370. Test during your busiest evening hours before cancelling any other service.
