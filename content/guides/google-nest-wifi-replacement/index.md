The best Nest Wifi replacement depends on one question: is your Google router also your smart home's Thread border router? If it is, and nothing else in your home fills that role, buy a mesh system with a Thread border router built in. eero's Wi-Fi 7 systems, including eero 7 and eero Pro 7, have one, and eero guarantees those two models security updates until at least October 31, 2031. If you want to keep managing Wi-Fi in the Google Home app, Nest Wifi Pro is Google's only current router, but its guaranteed updates end on October 27, 2027. And if you already own a Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max or Google TV Streamer, Thread is already covered, so any mesh system will do.

This guide is for owners of the original Google Wifi, the Wi-Fi 5 Nest Wifi, or Nest Wifi Pro who are planning a switch. It's based on manufacturer documentation and listings, not our own testing.

## Do you need to replace it yet?

Not urgently. On October 23, 2026, Google's security-update table lists all three of its Wi-Fi products as "currently receiving critical security updates." The catch is that only Nest Wifi Pro is still inside Google's guarantee:

| Model | Guaranteed updates until at least | Currently receiving critical security updates (Google) |
|---|---|---|
| Google Wifi | December 5, 2021 (passed) | Yes |
| Nest Wifi | October 28, 2024 (passed) | Yes |
| Nest Wifi Pro | October 27, 2027 | Yes |

Updates past the guaranteed date can stop without a fixed end date, so it makes sense to plan your replacement now. Our [Nest Wifi support status guide]({{< relref "/guides/google-nest-wifi-support-status" >}}) covers firmware versions and what each date means.

## Step 1: Work out whether your router handles Thread

Thread is the low-power mesh network many Matter smart home devices use, such as some locks, sensors and bulbs. Google says "a Thread Border Router is required" to set up a Thread device in the Google Home app, and the border router is also what lets you control those devices from your phone when you're away. (Our [Matter and Thread explainer]({{< relref "/guides/matter-thread-smart-home-explained" >}}) covers the basics.)

Google's Thread help page lists these Google devices as Thread border routers:

- Nest Wifi Pro (Wi-Fi 6E)
- Nest Hub (2nd gen)
- Nest Hub Max
- Google TV Streamer (4K)
- Google Home Speaker (2026)

What that means for you:

- **You own Nest Wifi Pro and no other device on that list:** your router is your only Google Thread border router. Replace it with something that has one built in, or add one of the other listed devices first.
- **You own a Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max, Google TV Streamer or Google Home Speaker (2026):** Thread is handled by that device. Your choice of Wi-Fi system doesn't affect it, as long as the device stays plugged in and connected.
- **You own the original Nest Wifi or Google Wifi:** neither is on Google's current Thread page. Google's product comparison page listed the Nest Wifi router and point as Thread border routers when we checked it for our October 22 support-status guide, but we couldn't recheck that page today, so treat the older Nest Wifi as a possible border router. If you use any Thread devices, make sure one of the devices listed above is in your home before you unplug the old system.
- **You don't use any Thread devices:** skip to Step 2.

Note that Google's guaranteed dates for the Nest Hub (2nd gen), March 30, 2026, and the Nest Hub Max, September 9, 2024, have also passed, though Google lists both as currently receiving critical security updates. The Google TV Streamer's guarantee runs to September 24, 2029.

### You need a Thread border router built in: eero

eero lists its current Wi-Fi 7 lineup, including eero 7, eero Pro 7, eero Max 7, eero PoE 7 and eero Outdoor 7, along with several Wi-Fi 6 and 6E models, as Thread border routers that are "Thread 1.3.0 compliant." It says "all Thread devices should be interoperable with eero's border router." eero's product page for the Pro 7 also describes a built-in smart home hub for Thread, Zigbee and Matter devices, with Matter support "as a controller only." (The same page's FAQ applies that limit to Zigbee too.)

The two models differ in speed and ports, according to eero's listings:

- **eero Pro 7:** tri-band, two 5 GbE Ethernet ports, wireless speeds up to 3.9 Gbps, for internet plans up to 5 Gbps.
- **eero 7:** two 2.5 GbE Ethernet ports, wireless speeds up to 1.8 Gbps, for internet plans up to 2.5 Gbps.

eero rates both at up to 2,000 square feet per unit; Google rates Nest Wifi Pro at up to 2,200 square feet per router. These are maximum link rates and coverage claims from the manufacturers, not measured speeds. For the full differences, including the eero Max 7, see our [eero 7 vs. eero Pro 7 vs. eero Max 7 comparison]({{< relref "/guides/eero-7-vs-eero-pro-7-vs-eero-max-7" >}}).

If you use an iPhone or Android phone, eero says its app can store Thread network credentials in your phone's keychain, synced through iCloud or Google accounts, which other apps can read with your permission. That helps when devices from different brands need to join the same Thread network.

### You want to stay in Google Home: Nest Wifi Pro

Nest Wifi Pro has what Google's store page calls a "built-in Thread border router" and is "built with Matter." It's still on sale on the US Google Store, from $199.99 for a single router when we checked on October 23, 2026.

The trade-off is support length. Buying one today gets you guaranteed updates for about a year, to October 27, 2027. Google has kept patching older Wi-Fi products past their guaranteed dates, but it hasn't promised to. Google also says Nest Wifi Pro is "not compatible with previous generations of Nest or Google Wifi routers or points," so you'd be replacing every unit, not adding one. Our comparison of the [most durable mesh Wi-Fi systems]({{< relref "/guides/most-durable-mesh-wifi" >}}) lines up the written update and warranty terms across brands.

### You already have a Thread border router: choose on coverage and support

If a Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max, Google TV Streamer or another border router is handling Thread, pick a mesh system on how well it covers your home and how long its maker commits to updates. That includes systems without Thread, such as TP-Link Deco. In a November 20, 2024 answer marked "Official" on TP-Link's community forum, a TP-Link representative said Deco "supports Matter over Wi-Fi/Ethernet but doesn't support thread." As of October 23, 2026, we found no later TP-Link statement saying otherwise.

## Step 3: Switch over without breaking your smart home

1. **List what depends on the old system.** Note your Wi-Fi name and password, any Thread devices, and any Nest Wifi points you use as speakers. The Nest Wifi point has a built-in speaker and Google Assistant. The new systems' listings we checked describe no built-in speaker, so budget for a separate smart speaker if you rely on the points for voice control.
2. **Make sure a Thread border router will stay on.** If your Nest router is your only one, set up the new border router, or a Nest Hub (2nd gen), Nest Hub Max or Google TV Streamer, before you remove it.
3. **Reuse your network name and password.** Devices that remember them may reconnect on their own. Anything that doesn't will need to be set up again in its own app.
4. **Check your Thread devices after the swap.** The Thread Group says that when border routers share Thread credentials, "all the Thread devices form part of the same Thread mesh network." If your new border router starts a separate Thread network instead, devices paired to the old one may need to be reset and paired again.
5. **Wire the satellites if you can.** Ethernet between mesh units frees up wireless capacity for your devices. Check your system's setup guide for how to connect them.
6. **Factory reset the old Google units** before you sell, recycle or give them away.
