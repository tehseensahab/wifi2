**Buy the Orbi 370 if you want NETGEAR's mesh on a budget and none of your devices use 6 GHz; buy the Orbi 770 if you have Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 devices, need more wired ports, or have more than 70 devices.** The 370 is dual-band Wi-Fi 7 (2.4 and 5 GHz only), while the 770 adds the 6 GHz band, more 2.5 Gig Ethernet ports and support for up to 100 devices. On November 25, 2026, during NETGEAR's Black Friday sale, a 370 2-pack was $189.99 at netgear.com and a 770 2-pack was $423.99, a $234 difference.

This comparison is based on NETGEAR's US product pages, its launch announcement and its own blog posts. We have not tested either system; coverage and speed figures below are NETGEAR's ratings, not our measurements.

## Orbi 370 vs. 770 at a glance

| | Orbi 370 (RBE372 2-pack) | Orbi 770 (RBE772 2-pack) |
|---|---|---|
| Wi-Fi bands | 2.4 + 5 GHz (dual-band) | 2.4 + 5 + 6 GHz (tri-band) |
| NETGEAR rating | BE5000, "up to 5 Gbps" | BE11000, "up to 11 Gbps" |
| Internet (WAN) port | 2.5 Gig | 2.5 Gig |
| LAN ports, router | 1 × 2.5 Gig | 3 × 2.5 Gig |
| LAN ports, each satellite | 1 × 2.5 Gig | 2 × 2.5 Gig |
| Devices | Up to 70 | Up to 100 |
| Coverage, 2-pack | Up to 4,000 sq ft | Up to 5,500 sq ft |
| MLO | Yes (2.4 + 5 GHz) | Yes |
| Separate backhaul-only band | No | No (NETGEAR: exclusive to Orbi 970) |
| netgear.com price, Nov. 25, 2026 | $189.99 (listed down from $249.99) | $423.99 (listed down from $549.99) |

The "up to 5 Gbps" and "up to 11 Gbps" ratings are maximum wireless signal rates that NETGEAR says are derived from the 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) specifications. A single phone or laptop won't reach that speed, and your internet plan still limits what you get from the internet.

## The real difference: the 6 GHz band

The Orbi 370 has no 6 GHz radio. NETGEAR's own Orbi 370 blog says "Higher-tier Orbi systems (like the 770 or 970 Series) use tri-band WiFi or quad-band WiFi technology, offering access to the super-fast 6Ghz WiFi band."

Whether that matters depends on your devices. A phone, laptop or TV that supports only Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 can't use 6 GHz anyway, so on a 770 it would connect over 2.4 or 5 GHz, just as it would on a 370. Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7 devices can use 6 GHz on the 770. Our guide to [what you lose with dual-band Wi-Fi 7](/guides/dual-band-wifi-7/) explains the trade-off in more detail, and you can [check whether your devices support 6 GHz](/guides/check-6ghz-wifi-support/).

The third band also matters for the mesh link itself. In a dual-band system like the 370, the satellites talk to the router over the same 2.4 and 5 GHz bands your devices use. NETGEAR's 370 blog lists "Options for wireless backhaul or wired backhaul via Ethernet cable LAN ports." The 770 has an extra band to spread traffic across, though NETGEAR's 770 page is clear that it isn't reserved for backhaul: "A separate band used only for backhaul is exclusive to Orbi 970." Our [tri-band vs. dual-band mesh](/guides/tri-band-vs-dual-band-mesh/) guide covers why that distinction matters, and [wired backhaul](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/) sidesteps it on either system.

Both systems support Wi-Fi 7's Multi-Link Operation. NETGEAR's launch release says the 370's MLO "combines 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands to boost speed, reduce latency, and increase performance," and its 770 page says the 770 "uses WiFi 7 Multi-Link Operation (MLO) across its tri-band design." See our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO guide](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) for what MLO does and doesn't do.

## Ports: one each vs. several

Both routers have a 2.5 Gig internet port, so on paper either one can take a plan faster than 1 Gbps from your modem. The difference is how many devices you can plug in:

- **Orbi 370:** the router has one 2.5 Gig LAN port, and each satellite has one 2.5 Gig LAN port.
- **Orbi 770:** the router has three 2.5 Gig LAN ports, and each satellite has two.

If you have a desktop PC, a game console and a TV near the router, the 370's single LAN port means buying a switch; the 770's three ports can take all three. If you only plan to plug in one device, or none, the 370's ports are enough.

## Which should you buy?

| If you... | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly have Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 devices and want the lowest price | **Orbi 370** | Your devices can't use 6 GHz, which is the 770's main extra |
| Have Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 phones, laptops or PCs | **Orbi 770** | Only the 770 has a 6 GHz band |
| Want to plug in several devices at the router or satellites | **Orbi 770** | Three LAN ports on the router and two per satellite, vs. one each on the 370 |
| Have more than 70 connected devices | **Orbi 770** | NETGEAR rates it for up to 100, vs. 70 for the 370 |
| Need a dedicated backhaul band | **Neither** | Look at the [Orbi 970](/guides/orbi-770-vs-870-vs-970/) |

## Subscriptions and warranty

Neither system requires an Armor subscription. Both product pages say NETGEAR Armor, a Bitdefender-powered security service, includes a free trial and that "a paid subscription is optional after the trial." Our guide to [whether NETGEAR Armor is worth it](/guides/is-netgear-armor-worth-it/) covers that choice.

Both product pages carry the heading "Security built in & 1-year warranty" and say the NETGEAR limited warranty is valid only if you buy from an authorized reseller and register the device.

## Bottom line

The Orbi 370 is the cheaper way into NETGEAR's Wi-Fi 7 mesh, and it's a sensible pick if your devices are mostly Wi-Fi 5 or Wi-Fi 6 and you only need one wired port at each point. The Orbi 770 costs a lot more, and you get your money's worth only if you'll use its 6 GHz band, its extra Ethernet ports or its higher device limit.
