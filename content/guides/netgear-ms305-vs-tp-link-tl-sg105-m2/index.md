The NETGEAR MS305 and the TP-Link TL-SG105-M2 are close to identical on paper: five 2.5 Gbps Ethernet ports, a fanless metal case and no setup. For most homes either one does the job of adding 2.5 Gbps wired ports next to a router. The differences that matter are the warranty and which hardware version you get: NETGEAR lists a limited lifetime warranty on the original MS305 and three years on the newer MS305v2, and the newer version of each switch has a much lower listed maximum power draw than the first.

This comparison is based on NETGEAR's and TP-Link's product pages and data sheets, checked on November 16, 2026. We have not tested either switch.

## The short version

| | NETGEAR MS305 | NETGEAR MS305v2 | TP-Link TL-SG105-M2 (US page) | TP-Link TL-SG105-M2 v3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ports | 5 × 100M/1G/2.5G | 5 × 100M/1G/2.5G | 5 × 100M/1G/2.5G | 5 × 100M/1G/2.5G |
| Switching capacity | Not listed | 25 Gbps | 25 Gbps | 25 Gbps |
| Cooling | Fanless | Fanless | Fanless | Fanless |
| Case | Metal | Metal | Metal | Metal |
| Power adapter | Not listed | External, 12 V / 1.5 A | External, 12 V / 1 A | External, 9 V / 0.6 A |
| Max power draw | 9.24 W | 5.57 W | 12.11 W | 5.64 W |
| Jumbo frames | 9,720 bytes | 12K bytes | 10 KB | 12 KB |
| Traffic priority (QoS) | 802.1p | 802.1p/CoS, DSCP | 802.1p/DSCP | 802.1p/DSCP |
| Warranty | Limited lifetime (NETGEAR) | 3 years (NETGEAR) | Not stated by TP-Link | Not stated by TP-Link |

"Not listed" means the maker's page we checked doesn't give the figure. TP-Link's US page doesn't name a hardware version; its specs match TP-Link's version 1 page, so treat that column as the original TL-SG105-M2.

## What's the same

Both are unmanaged switches: you plug in power and cables and they work, with no app or web page to set up. NETGEAR describes the MS305v2 as "Plug-and-play: no software, no setup," and TP-Link's page is headed "Just Plug and Play for Internet Access." Every port on both runs at 100 Mbps, 1 Gbps or 2.5 Gbps, picking the fastest speed the device on the other end supports.

Both are fanless, so they make no fan noise, and both use metal cases; the MS305v2 and TL-SG105-M2 pages list external power adapters. TP-Link and NETGEAR both list desktop or wall mounting. Neither has a port faster than 2.5 Gbps, so neither will pass more than 2.5 Gbps to a single device, and a 5 Gbps or 10 Gbps port on your router or modem will connect to either switch at 2.5 Gbps.

## Where they differ

### Warranty

This is the clearest difference. NETGEAR's page for the original MS305 says it is "backed by NETGEAR limited lifetime hardware warranty." The MS305v2 page lists a "3-year warranty, backed by NETGEAR," and its data sheet a "3-year limited hardware warranty," with two years on the power adapter. TP-Link's US product page links to its warranty section but doesn't state a term for this switch. Micro Center's listing for the TL-SG105-M2 shows a three-year limited warranty, but that is a retailer listing, not TP-Link's own statement, so check the box or TP-Link's warranty page before you rely on it.

### Power draw

NETGEAR lists the original MS305 at 9.24 W maximum and the MS305v2 at 5.57 W. TP-Link's US page lists the TL-SG105-M2 at 12.11 W maximum, while its hardware version 3.0 data sheet lists 5.64 W. These are maximum figures from the makers, not measurements of typical use. At the maximum, the gap between 12.11 W and 5.64 W is about 6.5 W, or about 57 kWh a year if a switch ran flat out around the clock; real use would be lower.

### Hardware versions

Both makers have changed these switches without changing the name much, so check the box or listing before you buy:

- **NETGEAR:** The MS305v2 is listed on its own page. Its data sheet gives the North American SKU as MS305-200NAS. A retailer listing for MS305-100NAS, such as CDW's on November 16, 2026, is the original MS305.
- **TP-Link:** The model name stays TL-SG105-M2. TP-Link's version 1 page and its version 3.0 data sheet list different power adapters (12 V / 1 A vs. 9 V / 0.6 A), different maximum power (12.11 W vs. 5.64 W) and different jumbo frame limits (10 KB vs. 12 KB). Look for the hardware version on the listing or the box, then read the data sheet for that version.

### Traffic priority

The MS305v2 and the TL-SG105-M2 both list support for 802.1p and DSCP, the tags routers and access points can use to mark traffic such as video calls as higher priority. NETGEAR says the MS305v2 "Honors 802.1p and DSCP tags from upstream routers and access points." NETGEAR's page for the original MS305 lists 802.1p QoS but not DSCP. In an unmanaged switch you can't change these settings; it only follows tags already on the traffic.

## Price and availability (checked November 16, 2026)

On November 16, 2026, netgear.com showed the MS305v2 as "Out of Stock." NETGEAR's page for the original MS305 showed no price or stock status in its page text. TP-Link's US product page showed the TL-SG105-M2 as "Coming Soon" with no price, though the page links to Amazon, and Micro Center's listing said "Sorry, this item is no longer available." Business reseller CDW listed both in stock, at prices aimed at business buyers, so we haven't used them as a guide to what you'll pay at a consumer retailer. Prices and stock change; check at the time you buy, and check the hardware version on the listing.

## Which should you buy?

- **NETGEAR MS305 (original):** You want the longest warranty. In North America, look for SKU MS305-100NAS on the listing; MS305-200NAS is the v2.
- **NETGEAR MS305v2:** You want the lower-power version with a published 3-year warranty and DSCP priority support, and you can find it in stock.
- **TP-Link TL-SG105-M2:** It's the one in stock at a good price where you shop. If power draw matters to you, check the hardware version and look up that version's data sheet.

Before you buy any 2.5G switch, check that the devices you'll plug in have 2.5 Gbps ports and that your plan is faster than 1 Gbps; our guide to [whether you need a multi-gig router](/guides/do-you-need-a-multi-gig-router/) walks through that. The cable already in your walls is probably fine: see [can Cat 6 handle 2.5 Gbps](/guides/can-cat6-handle-2-5-gbps/). A switch is also a common way to add wired ports for [Ethernet backhaul in a mesh system](/guides/mesh-wifi-wired-vs-wireless-backhaul/), and if you're still choosing the router itself, see our [best Wi-Fi router for a large house guide](/guides/best-wifi-router-for-large-house/).
