**The Orbi 370 and eero 7 are the same class of mesh system: dual-band Wi-Fi 7 (2.4 and 5 GHz only), BE5000-rated, with Multi-Link Operation and 2.5 Gig ports. Buy the Orbi 370 if price matters most; buy the eero 7 if you need two wired ports on every satellite, have more than 70 devices, or want a longer warranty.** On November 26, 2026, an Orbi 370 2-pack was $189.99 at netgear.com (on sale from $249.99) and an eero 7 2-pack was $284.99 at eero.com, a $95 difference.

This comparison is based on NETGEAR's and eero's US product and support pages. We have not tested either system; coverage, device and speed figures are the makers' ratings, not our measurements.

## What's the same

On every spec that decides how fast and how far your Wi-Fi reaches, these two match:

- **No 6 GHz.** NETGEAR lists the Orbi 370's bands as "2.4 + 5 GHz." eero calls the eero 7 "the dual-band Wi-Fi 7 router" that "uses the 2.4 and 5 GHz bands." In practice, your Wi-Fi 6E and Wi-Fi 7 devices will connect on 5 GHz with either system. Our [dual-band Wi-Fi 7 explainer](/guides/dual-band-wifi-7/) covers what that gives up.
- **Same rating.** Both carry a BE5000 rating. That's a class label for the combined maximum signal rate across both bands, not a speed one device will get.
- **MLO.** NETGEAR lists "Multi-Link Operation (MLO)" as an Orbi 370 Series feature, and eero lists "Multi-Link operation (MLO) support" for the eero 7.
- **Coverage per unit.** NETGEAR rates each Orbi 370 unit for "up to 2,000 sq. ft."; eero rates the eero 7 at "up to 2,000 sq. ft. (190 m²) of indoor coverage per unit." So a 2-pack of either is rated around 4,000 sq ft and a 3-pack around 6,000 sq ft. These are maker estimates; walls, floors and placement decide real coverage.
- **2.5 Gig internet port.** Both can take a multi-gig connection from your modem or ONT. eero says the eero 7 "supports internet plans up to 2.5 Gbps."
- **WPA3.** NETGEAR's pages mention WPA3 encryption; eero's announcement lists "WPA3 and WPA2 wireless encryption."

### Why the speed numbers look different

NETGEAR advertises the Orbi 370 as "up to 5 Gbps," while eero's shop page says the eero 7 has "wireless speeds up to 1.8 Gbps." Both are BE5000 systems. NETGEAR's "up to 5 Gbps" appears to restate that BE5000 rating; eero publishes a separate 1.8 Gbps wireless figure and doesn't say how it was derived. Don't use either number to pick between them, and don't expect either from a single device.

## Where they differ

### Price

The Orbi 370 is cheaper right now. On November 26, 2026, netgear.com listed the RBE372 2-pack at $189.99 and the RBE373 3-pack at $274.99, both shown below their original prices during NETGEAR's Black Friday sale. eero.com listed the eero 7 at $169.99 for one unit, $284.99 for a 2-pack and $399.99 for a 3-pack. That's $95 more for the eero 2-pack and $125 more for the 3-pack.

Sale prices move. If you're shopping later, check both again: at NETGEAR's list prices ($249.99 and $349.99) the gap shrinks to $35 for the 2-pack and $50 for the 3-pack.

### Wired ports

This is the most practical difference. Each eero 7 has "two auto-sensing 2.5 GbE ports," so either port can serve as the internet port. On the main eero that leaves one spare port, the same as the Orbi 370 router (one WAN plus one LAN). On the satellites, though, every extra eero 7 has two 2.5 GbE ports, while each Orbi 370 satellite has one.

If you want to plug in a TV and a game console next to a satellite, the eero 7 handles it without a switch (assuming the satellite connects wirelessly; a wired backhaul uses one port). With the Orbi 370 you'd add an inexpensive Ethernet switch.

### Device count

NETGEAR says the Orbi 370 Series "is designed to handle up to 70 connections" at once. eero says the eero 7 "can support 120+ devices." Seventy covers many households, but homes with many smart plugs, cameras and sensors can get there. These are maker ratings, not tested limits.

### Warranty

eero lists "a limited 3-year warranty" for the eero 7. NETGEAR's Orbi 370 pages advertise a "1-year warranty" and say the limited warranty is valid only when bought from an authorized NETGEAR reseller and the device is registered.

### Subscriptions and app

Neither system needs a subscription. NETGEAR includes a free trial of NETGEAR Armor and says "a paid subscription is optional after the trial." eero says the eero 7 "is compatible with eero Plus," a separate paid service. See [Is eero Plus worth it?](/guides/is-eero-plus-worth-it/) and [Is NETGEAR Armor worth it?](/guides/is-netgear-armor-worth-it/) for what each adds.

NETGEAR says Orbi 370 setup requires the Orbi iOS or Android app and a free NETGEAR account.

## Which should you buy?

- **Lower price of these two:** Orbi 370, at current sale prices.
- **Wired devices at each satellite (TV, console, desktop):** eero 7, for its two ports per unit.
- **Lots of smart-home devices:** eero 7, rated for 120+ versus 70.
- **Longer warranty:** eero 7 (3 years vs. 1).
- **You have Wi-Fi 6E or Wi-Fi 7 devices and want 6 GHz:** neither. Look at a tri-band system such as the [Orbi 770](/guides/orbi-370-vs-770/).
- **Upgrading from an older eero:** our [eero 6+ vs. eero 7](/guides/eero-6-plus-vs-eero-7/) comparison covers that move. For the brands in general, see [eero vs. Orbi](/guides/eero-vs-orbi/).
