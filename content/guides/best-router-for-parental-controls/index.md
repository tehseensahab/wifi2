The best router for parental controls depends on what you want to pay and how hands-on you want to be. **eero** has the simplest app, but its content filters and app blocking need an eero Plus subscription ($9.99 a month in the US). **ASUS** says its routers include parental controls with no subscription, though setup is more technical and the features vary by model. **TP-Link** has the cheapest add-on plan we could price, at $2.99 a month or $17.99 a year, on top of a useful free tier.

This guide is based on the companies' own support pages, pricing pages and product listings, checked on October 30, 2026. We haven't tested these routers ourselves.

## The short version

| If you want... | Look at | What's free | What costs extra (US) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The simplest app, with filters if you pay | eero (for example, eero 7) | Profiles, scheduled pauses, pausing a device | eero Plus: $9.99/month or $99.99/year for content filters, app blocking, custom site lists |
| Filtering and schedules with no subscription | ASUS (for example, RT-BE58U) | Parental controls and AiProtection, per ASUS; features vary by model | Nothing, per ASUS |
| The cheapest paid upgrade we could price | TP-Link Archer or Deco with HomeShield | Profiles, website blocking, category filters, basic bedtime | Advanced Parental Controls: $2.99/month or $17.99/year for time limits, app blocking, SafeSearch |
| To stay with NETGEAR Nighthawk or Orbi | NETGEAR Smart Parental Controls | Not described in NETGEAR's help article | Premium for content filtering and off times; price shown in the app |

## What router parental controls can and can't do

A router sees every device on your Wi-Fi, so its controls also cover game consoles, smart TVs and laptops that don't have their own parental settings. That's the main reason to use the router for this rather than only phone settings.

The limits are just as important:

- **They only cover your network.** A phone on cellular data, or a laptop at a friend's house, is outside the router's reach. The parental controls built into the device itself, such as Apple's Screen Time or Google's Family Link, keep working there.
- **Changing Wi-Fi addresses can break profiles.** Router controls usually recognize a device by its Wi-Fi MAC address. eero's support page says randomized addresses can affect "Profile assignment and schedules" and "Pausing and blocking devices." eero also writes that when a device forgets the network and rejoins, "the device generates a new random MAC address," so it can look like a new, unassigned device. Our guide to [private Wi-Fi addresses]({{< relref "/guides/private-wifi-address" >}}) explains when to turn this off for a child's device.
- **New devices need a default rule.** Look for a setting that applies limits to any device not assigned to a profile. eero calls this Unassigned Device Control. Seeing [who's connected to your Wi-Fi]({{< relref "/guides/see-who-is-connected-to-my-wifi" >}}) regularly also helps.

## The picks in detail

### eero: the easiest to manage, if you pay for filtering

eero says "Every eero system comes with core Parental Controls built right into the app at no additional cost." In its July 21, 2026 article, those core tools include profiles for each family member, scheduled Wi-Fi pauses (for example, during meals or bedtime), pausing a single device, and notifications when a new device joins.

What eero doesn't include for free is filtering. Content filters (including category blocking, safe search and YouTube Restricted Mode), blocking specific apps for a profile, and custom blocked and allowed sites all need eero Plus. eero lists the US price as "$9.99 monthly / $99.99 yearly." eero Plus also covers features unrelated to parental controls, such as ad blocking.

If you're buying new hardware, the eero 7 was $169.99 for one unit and $284.99 for a two-pack on eero's US store on October 30, 2026. Our [eero 7 vs. eero Pro 7 vs. eero Max 7 guide]({{< relref "/guides/eero-7-vs-eero-pro-7-vs-eero-max-7" >}}) covers which model fits your home.

**Best for:** parents who want to manage everything from a phone app and are fine with a subscription for filtering.

### ASUS: filtering without a subscription, with more setup

ASUS's AiProtection page states: "No, ASUS AiProtection is completely subscription-free." Its comparison table lists parental controls as "Built-in Free." That page is ASUS's UK site; we didn't find an equivalent US page, so treat the "free" claim as ASUS's general statement rather than a US-specific promise.

What you get depends on the model. ASUS's US help article describes two features: Web & Apps Filters, which "block access to unwanted websites and apps," and Time Scheduling for specific devices. It also notes that Web & Apps Filters is "only supported on selected models." The article was last updated March 3, 2026.

As an example, ASUS's Canadian spec page for the Wi-Fi 7 RT-BE58U lists "Time Scheduling, Safe Browsing, Customized Internet Schedule" under parental controls. Adorama's listing for the same model also lists "Web & Apps Filters." Adorama had the RT-BE58U at $149.99 ("Limited Quantity Available") on October 30, 2026. Check the spec page for the exact model you're buying.

**Best for:** parents comfortable with a router's web settings who would rather not pay every month.

### TP-Link: a useful free tier and the cheapest upgrade

TP-Link's parental controls are part of HomeShield, managed in the Deco app for Deco mesh systems or the Tether app for Archer routers. TP-Link's setup guide (updated May 6, 2026) shows these working without a subscription: separate profiles for each child with age-based presets, blocking specific websites, filtering content by category, and a basic "No Internet" bedtime.

The paid tier, Advanced Parental Controls, adds SafeSearch and YouTube Restricted Mode, app blocking, online time limits, bedtimes that vary by day, off-time schedules, time rewards and Family Time. TP-Link lists US prices of $2.99 a month or $17.99 a year, with a one-month free trial for new users (pricing page updated May 11, 2026).

For example, TP-Link's product page for the Archer BE230 lists HomeShield parental controls including custom profiles, bedtime, off time and time rewards. Its footnote reads: "HomeShield includes the Free Basic Plan. Fees apply for the Pro Plan." TP-Link uses "Pro Plan" there and "Advanced Parental Controls" on its pricing page, so check which features are free for your model in the app.

If you're weighing TP-Link because of the [state lawsuits against the company]({{< relref "/news/tp-link-state-lawsuits-what-owners-should-know" >}}), read that first.

**Best for:** budget-minded parents who want time limits and app blocking without paying eero Plus prices.

### NETGEAR: only if you're staying with Nighthawk or Orbi

NETGEAR's help article says its Smart Parental Controls Premium plan, started from the Nighthawk or Orbi app, "gives you access to exciting features like the My Time app, scheduled off times (no Internet access), and content filtering." The article (last updated July 7, 2025) doesn't list a price; it tells you to tap **See Pricing** in the app. We couldn't confirm the current US price, so we can't compare it with the others. If you already own a recent Nighthawk or Orbi, check the app before buying anything new.

## How to choose

1. **List the devices you want to control.** If it's mainly phones, use the controls built into the phone; a router won't help on cellular data. If it includes consoles, TVs or a shared PC, router controls are worth having.
2. **Decide which features you need.** A basic schedule or bedtime is free on all three main options, but TP-Link charges for off-time schedules and bedtimes that vary by day. Basic content filtering is free on supported ASUS models and TP-Link (category filters), but needs eero Plus on eero. App blocking costs extra on eero and TP-Link, and TP-Link also charges for time limits.
3. **Count the yearly cost.** eero Plus is $99.99 a year; TP-Link's Advanced Parental Controls is $17.99 a year; ASUS says it charges nothing.
4. **Check the exact model and plan.** ASUS says some features are only on selected models, and TP-Link says some features need its paid plan. eero says its core controls come with every eero system.
5. **Set a default rule for unknown devices,** and keep the router's firmware current; our [router hardening checklist]({{< relref "/guides/router-hardening-checklist" >}}) covers the rest.
