The best Wi-Fi 7 adapter for a PC depends on two things: whether you can open the case, and which version of Windows you run. For a laptop, or a desktop with no free slot, get a USB adapter: the NETGEAR Nighthawk A9000 on Windows 11, or the TP-Link Archer TBE6500U if the PC is still on Windows 10. For a desktop with a free PCIe slot, the TP-Link Archer TBE550E card has the higher rated 6 GHz speed and adds Bluetooth 5.4. Before you buy any of them, check that you have Windows 11 version 24H2 or later and a Wi-Fi 7 router, because Microsoft says that's what Wi-Fi 7 needs.

This is a research-based guide. We haven't tested these adapters: the specs and requirements below come from Microsoft, NETGEAR and TP-Link, and the one price we list was checked on November 5, 2026. Speeds are the makers' maximum link rates, not the throughput you'd see at home.

## Quick picks by need

| Your PC | Pick | Connection | Rated max link rate (6 GHz / 5 GHz / 2.4 GHz) | Windows listed by maker |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laptop or desktop, Windows 11 | NETGEAR Nighthawk A9000 | USB 3.0, with cable and cradle | 2,882 / 2,882 / 688 Mbps | Windows 11 only |
| Laptop or desktop, still on Windows 10 | TP-Link Archer TBE6500U | USB 3.0 Type-A | 2,882 / 2,882 / 688 Mbps | Windows 10 and 11 |
| Desktop with a free PCIe slot | TP-Link Archer TBE550E | PCIe card, external antenna base | 5,764 / 2,882 / 688 Mbps | Windows 11 (64-bit) only |

The two USB adapters have the same rated speeds on paper. The difference that matters is the operating system each maker supports, plus NETGEAR's cradle, which lets you put the adapter somewhere with a clearer path to the router instead of behind the PC.

## First, check whether you need an adapter at all

Your PC may already have Wi-Fi 7. Microsoft's check takes a few seconds: open Command Prompt or Terminal, run `netsh wlan show drivers`, and look at "Radio types supported." If it lists 802.11be, the PC already supports Wi-Fi 7, and an adapter won't add anything. If it stops at 802.11ax, your hardware is Wi-Fi 6 or 6E.

Then check the other two requirements Microsoft lists:

- **Windows 11, version 24H2 or later.** Microsoft says "Wi-Fi 7 is available starting with Windows 11, version 24H2," so a PC on Windows 10 or an earlier Windows 11 version won't get it. To check your version, go to Settings > System > About.
- **A Wi-Fi 7 router that supports WPA3.** An adapter can only use Wi-Fi 7 features the router also supports. TP-Link's footnotes say its adapters' Wi-Fi 7 features require "the AP and PC OS" to support them too. If your router is Wi-Fi 6 or 6E, a Wi-Fi 7 adapter will connect, but only with the features both ends share. Our [Wi-Fi 6E vs. Wi-Fi 7 guide](/guides/wifi-6e-vs-wifi-7/) covers whether the router upgrade is worth it.

One more check: whether faster Wi-Fi will help at all. An adapter speeds up the link between the PC and the router. It doesn't change your internet plan. If your plan is 500 Mbps and your PC already gets close to that over Wi-Fi, a new adapter won't move your speed test much. Test both before you buy: a speed test on the PC over Wi-Fi, then one over Ethernet if you can.

## NETGEAR Nighthawk A9000: USB pick for Windows 11

The A9000 is NETGEAR's Wi-Fi 7 USB adapter, sold as the "Nighthawk WiFi 7 USB 3.0 Adapter (for Windows 11 PC)." It was **$89.99 at netgear.com on November 5, 2026**; the store page didn't show stock status.

**What NETGEAR lists:**

- Tri-band Wi-Fi 7 (BE6500): up to 688 Mbps on 2.4 GHz, 2,882 Mbps on 5 GHz and 2,882 Mbps on 6 GHz.
- USB 3.0. NETGEAR says it gives "best performance with USB 3.0" and is "compatible with 2.0," so plug it into a USB 3.0 port if you have one.
- In the box: a USB 3.0 cable with a cradle, an installation USB thumb drive and a quick start guide.
- "WiFi 7 features & 6 GHz require Windows 11," and it's "Not compatible with Windows 10 or older."

**Who it's for:** a Windows 11 laptop or desktop where you'd rather not open the case. The cradle is the practical advantage: a USB adapter plugged straight into the back of a desktop under a desk has metal and furniture between it and the router. On a cable, you can put it on the desk.

**What to know:** NETGEAR's product page mentions "320MHz high-capacity channels," but its data sheet doesn't mention 320 MHz or MLO, and the product page doesn't mention MLO. Its rated 6 GHz maximum (2,882 Mbps) is the same as its 5 GHz figure, and half the TBE550E's 5,764 Mbps; TP-Link lists 320 MHz support for the TBE550E on 6 GHz. Don't buy the A9000 expecting the TBE550E's rated 6 GHz speed. NETGEAR also says the 6 GHz band "is limited to indoor range."

## TP-Link Archer TBE6500U: USB pick if you're still on Windows 10

The Archer TBE6500U is TP-Link's BE6500 tri-band Wi-Fi 7 USB adapter. We didn't find a US retailer price we could verify today, so we've left the price out; TP-Link's US page links to Best Buy.

**What TP-Link lists:**

- Up to 2,882 Mbps on 6 GHz, 2,882 Mbps on 5 GHz and 688 Mbps on 2.4 GHz, the same rated speeds as the A9000.
- Multi-Link Operation (MLO) is listed as a feature.
- One USB 3.0 Type-A connection. The package contents list the adapter and a quick installation guide; TP-Link doesn't list a cable or cradle.
- "Compatible with Windows 11 and 10 on desktops and laptops." TP-Link adds that it "supports 6GHz on Windows 10 22H2 and later versions, though functionality and performance may be limited."

**Who it's for:** a PC that can't move to Windows 11 yet, but that you want ready for a future upgrade. It's the only adapter in this guide whose maker lists Windows 10.

**What to know:** Windows 10 support isn't Wi-Fi 7 support. Microsoft says Wi-Fi 7 starts with Windows 11 24H2, so on Windows 10 don't expect Wi-Fi 7 features such as MLO, and TP-Link itself warns 6 GHz may be limited. You'd be buying it for when the PC moves to Windows 11 24H2. TP-Link's channel-width footnote mentions 160 MHz, not 320 MHz.

## TP-Link Archer TBE550E: PCIe pick for desktops

The Archer TBE550E is a PCIe card for desktops, listed in TP-Link's US lineup as a "BE9300 Wi-Fi 7 Bluetooth 5.4 PCIe Adapter." TP-Link's US page doesn't show a price, and we didn't verify one today.

**What TP-Link lists:**

- Up to 5,764 Mbps on 6 GHz, 2,882 Mbps on 5 GHz and 688 Mbps on 2.4 GHz. TP-Link says these are physical rates derived from the IEEE 802.11 specifications.
- 320 MHz channels, which TP-Link says are "only available on the 6 GHz band," and may be unavailable in some regions.
- Multi-Link Operation (MLO).
- Bluetooth 5.4, with a 0.3 m Bluetooth header cable in the box. TP-Link doesn't say where the cable connects; on most desktops that's an internal USB header on the motherboard, so check that yours has a free one if you want Bluetooth.
- Two tri-band antennas on a magnetized base with a 1 m braided cable, so the antennas can sit on top of the desk rather than behind the case.
- "Supported operating system: Windows 11(64-bit) only."

**Who it's for:** a Windows 11 desktop with a free PCIe slot, near a Wi-Fi 7 router that supports 320 MHz channels on 6 GHz. That's the setup where its higher rated 6 GHz speed can come into play. If your router doesn't offer 320 MHz, the card's extra headroom goes unused, and a USB adapter would do the same job without opening the case.

**What to know:** TP-Link's US lineup includes other Wi-Fi 7 cards. The Archer TBE400E is rated BE6500; the TBE552E and TBE553E share the BE9300 rating but aren't the same card, so check each model's own spec page rather than assuming identical specs.

## What to skip

- **Any of these adapters for a Mac.** Neither NETGEAR nor TP-Link lists macOS support for these models.
- **A Wi-Fi 7 adapter for a PC on a Wi-Fi 6 router you don't plan to replace.** It will work, but at Wi-Fi 6 features. Spend the money on the router first, or use a Wi-Fi 6E adapter if you already have a 6E router .
- **Buying on the headline number.** "BE9300" and "BE6500" are roughly the sum of every band's maximum rate (5,764 + 2,882 + 688 = 9,334 Mbps for the TBE550E). A PC connects on one band at a time unless MLO is in use, and real throughput is lower than the link rate.

## After you install it

1. Install the maker's driver. TP-Link's TBE6500U product page notes that drivers may need updating after Windows updates.
2. Connect to your router's 6 GHz network, or its MLO network if it has one. 6 GHz needs WPA3 on the router; if the 6 GHz network doesn't appear, see [6 GHz Wi-Fi not showing up](/guides/6ghz-wifi-not-showing/).
3. Check the connection: in Settings > Network & internet > Wi-Fi, open your network's properties. Microsoft says that if **Network band (channel)** lists more than one band, such as 5 GHz and 6 GHz, MLO is supported on that connection. Our [Wi-Fi 7 MLO guide](/guides/wifi-7-mlo/) explains when MLO helps.

## Bottom line

Check that your PC doesn't already have Wi-Fi 7, and that it runs Windows 11 24H2 with a Wi-Fi 7 router to connect to. If all three check out, a USB adapter is the simple upgrade: the NETGEAR A9000 for Windows 11, with a cradle to get it out from behind the PC. A desktop with a free slot and a router that does 320 MHz on 6 GHz is the case for the TP-Link Archer TBE550E. The TP-Link Archer TBE6500U is for a PC that's stuck on Windows 10 for now, with the understanding that its Wi-Fi 7 features wait for Windows 11 24H2.
