**Xfinity's Storm-Ready support page now describes the Xfinity Pro WiFi Extender, and it's worth paying for only if you lease an Xfinity XB8, XB9 or XB10 gateway and can't afford to lose internet during an outage.** It's included with Xfinity Pro, a $15-a-month add-on, and can't be bought separately. When Xfinity's network goes down, the extender switches your Wi-Fi to 4G LTE at up to 30 Mbps down and 7 Mbps up. For power cuts, you need the optional $60 Battery Backup, good for up to four hours.

This guide is based on Xfinity's and Comcast's own pages, checked on November 11, 2026; we haven't tested the device. Prices are Xfinity's listed US prices and can change.

## What changed since Storm-Ready WiFi launched

Comcast launched Storm-Ready WiFi in August 2023 "for the low price of $7 a month for 36 months," with "a four-hour rechargeable battery" and LTE backup. Its December 2023 winter promotion put the full price at $252.

Today, Xfinity's Storm-Ready support page describes a different product: the Xfinity Pro WiFi Extender. Xfinity says it "is included with Xfinity Pro" and is "only available to eligible Xfinity Internet customers who subscribe to Xfinity Pro." The battery is now a separate purchase.

## What Xfinity Pro includes and costs

Xfinity's Xfinity Pro page says, "Add it to any Xfinity Internet plan for just $15/mo." For that you get:

- **The Pro WiFi Extender**, which "connects to a 4G LTE backup signal in a network outage to keep you online."
- **Wi-Fi extenders if Xfinity recommends them.** The page says "Max. up to 2 WiFi extenders (if recommended)" and that extenders are included at no additional cost.
- **Eligibility for the XB8 gateway.** Xfinity says, "All Xfinity Pro customers are eligible for our latest gateway, the XB8, regardless of their internet speed tier."

The **Battery Backup** is extra: a "$60 one-time charge." Xfinity says the cellular backup itself comes at no additional charge. Its current pages don't mention a data cap on the backup connection.

## How the backup works

**Network outage (power still on).** If Xfinity's cable service drops, the extender "automatically switches from broadband connection to a 4G LTE cellular connection." Xfinity says speeds "may be reduced to up to 30 Mbps download and 7 Mbps upload" and that "actual speeds may vary and aren't guaranteed." That's well below the speeds of many Xfinity plans, so treat it as a lifeline for lighter use, not a replacement for your full connection. Our [internet speed guide](/guides/how-much-internet-speed-do-i-need/) shows what typical tasks need.

**Power outage.** Without the battery, the extender is plugged into the wall like everything else and goes dark. With it, Xfinity says the battery keeps "the Pro WiFi Extender running for up to four hours during a power outage when fully charged." Xfinity's pages describe the battery powering the extender, not the gateway, so plan on staying within range of the extender during a power cut.

The LTE backup also depends on coverage: Xfinity says you need to be in "an area covered by the Verizon LTE network." Our [power outage guide](/guides/internet-power-outage/) covers how provider networks and cell sites have fared in past storms.

## Who can get it

Xfinity lists several conditions:

- **You need a compatible Xfinity gateway.** Xfinity says customers "who lease an Xfinity Gateway are eligible to add Xfinity Pro," and the extender "works with Xfinity Gateway XB8, XB9, or XB10 devices only." The gateway must be "not in Bridge Mode."
- **One gateway per home.** It's "not compatible with homes with more than one active Xfinity WiFi Gateway or modem." Xfinity also lists WiFi Ready Apartments and fiber-to-the-unit buildings as not compatible.
- **Post-paid service only.** Xfinity Pro "requires post-paid Xfinity Internet and compatible gateway."
- **Older extenders come out.** First-generation Xfinity WiFi Extenders "must be removed before you activate the Pro WiFi Extender."
- **No Xfinity Mobile needed.** Xfinity's FAQ says, "You don't need Xfinity Mobile service."

Setup is self-install: Xfinity says you can "set up and activate the Pro WiFi Extender yourself using the Xfinity app."

That gateway rule is the big one. If you [bought your own modem](/guides/best-modem-for-xfinity/) or run your own router with the gateway in [bridge mode](/guides/bridge-mode-double-nat-own-router/), Xfinity Pro doesn't fit your setup.

## Is it worth it?

**Worth it if:**

- You work from home, lease an XB8, XB9 or XB10 and your area has outages several times a year.
- You'd also use the included extenders to fix weak spots, if Xfinity recommends them for your home.
- You want backup that works without any setup when the cable line drops, and Verizon LTE is strong at your house.

**Skip it if:**

- You own your modem, or run the gateway in bridge mode for your own router or mesh system. You'd need a leased XB8, XB9 or XB10 not in bridge mode.
- Your phone has a good hotspot plan. [Using your phone as backup internet](/guides/phone-backup-internet-router/) may cover short outages within your data allowance.
- You mainly worry about power cuts, not cable outages. A [UPS for your modem and router](/guides/best-ups-for-router/) is a one-time cost and keeps your own equipment running while Xfinity's network still has power.
- You'd rather own your backup hardware. Router-based options like [eero Signal](/guides/eero-signal-4g-vs-5g/) or a [dual-WAN router](/guides/best-dual-wan-router/) work with any provider.

One early review is worth knowing about: Cord Cutters News reported in April 2026 that setup "was far more difficult than it should have been" and that the "extra $15 monthly fee for WiFi Pro with cellular backup feels a bit steep." That's one customer's experience, not a pattern we can confirm.
